The sample includes five emerging markets of Asia named; China, Pakistan, Hong Kong, Sri Lanka, and India. The asymmetric volatility spillover among the stock markets is examined using an extended EGARCH model. The results highlight certain interesting key findings. We find bidirectional volatility spillover between stock markets of India and Sri Lanka in both sub-periods. IntroductionDue to technological developments and financial deregulations, stock markets have become increasingly globalized and integration. There are many benefits of globalized stock markets such as decrease in cost of capital, and increase in real investment, and economic growth.1 The ample evidence show the benefit of globalized stock market, however, there are also costs associated with liberalized stock markets. The most important issue for policy makers about opening stock market for foreign investors is the “come and go” nature of the foreign investors. This nature may lead to the volatile capital flows and may result in increase stock market volatility.2 In addition Prasad, Rogoff, Wei, and Kose3 argued that integrations of equity markets can harm emerging economies because they will be more vulnerable to fluctuation of global financial markets.Several studies documented the potential cost of integrated markets. The integration of financial markets indicates the absence of potential diversification opportunities. In addition, the flow of financial shocks from one market to other market is high in integrated markets. Srivastava, Bhatia, and Gupta4 suggested that the massive and long-lasting effect of 1997 Asian crisis and 2007 subprime was probably due to increased stock market integrations. Furthermore Bae and Zhang1 suggested negative relation between stock market integration and a crisis period. They suggested two possible reasons for financial loss in a crisis situation. First, the sudden outflow of money by foreign investor, because they can easily leave integrated markets. Second, the risk exposure, as the integrated market will likely to face bigger loss due to financial contagion in a crisis situation.The empirical finance literature is rich with studies investigating the relationship between the financial markets; our study differentiates from the 3 aspects. First, the increasing role of emerging markets is becoming more important for investors and policy makers.5 The information about linkages between the emerging markets will provide valuable information to investors, which may help in portfolio formulation. Second, this study will investigate integrations among the stock markets considering pre and post financial crisis period, which will provide valuable information the linkages in the sub-periods. Third, we investigate both return and asymmetric volatility spillover among the financial markets, whereas most of the previous studies examined only returns linkages and transmission.The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. The next section review brief literature. The third section presents the methodology. The fourth section discusses the results. The last section concludes and recommends.2. Review of literatureIn finance literature, voluminous studies have been devoted in exploring integration and spillover effect among the stock markets. Several studies investigated the correlations among markets, while of the studies explored volatility spillover among the markets. Besides exploring the integration among the stock markets, most of the researchers have shed light on some special events like market liberalization, and market crisis on spillover of information across borders.The earlier studies show weak integration and spillover effect among the equity markets. Studies like6, 7, 8 reported weak integration among stock markets. However the most recent studies documented strong interdependencies among stock markets see for example Refs. 5, 9, 10, 11, 12. The strong interdependence among the stock markets is considered due to technological development and financial deregulations of financial markets.Most of the researchers paid their attentions to study the developed markets of US, UK, Canada, Germany, France, and Japan. The integrations of US with other equity markets remained an important subject in finance literature. Karolyi13 explored the shocks of return and volatility from New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) to Toronto Stock Exchange (TSE) of Canada and found weak linkages by using MGARCH model. Ng14 studied spillover of stock returns and volatility shocks from Japan and US to six Pacific Basin stock markets (Thailand, Taiwan, Hong Kong, Singapore and Malaysia) by using ARCH model. They find that local markets are integrated with the regional and global markets. The results showed that the volatility spillover effect from the global markets is greater than the regional markets. Apart from this, several studies like Refs. 9, 10, 11, 15, 16, 17, 18, 19, 20 have investigated the volatility spillover effect among the developed and emerging stock markets.Most of the studies were contributed in studying the integrations among Asian markets. Pan, Liu, and Roth21 examined the long run dynamics of returns and volatility linkages for stock return traded on the U.S and Asia–Pacific stock returns (Hong Kong, Japan, Malaysia, Singapore and Australia). The results indicated that all the six markets are highly integrated in second moments of returns but not in the first moments. Worthington and Higgs22 explored the volatility spillover effect among the Asian developed markets (Hong Kong, Singapore, and Japan) and emerging markets (Indonesia, Malaysia, Thailand, Taiwan and Philippines). They found that spillover from developed to emerging stock market were not homogeneous. They found that stock market volatility is higher in own markets than across markets, mostly for emerging equity markets. Mukherjee and Mishra18 tried to find the linkage between India with its major Asian counterparts (Korea, Hong Kong, Singapore, and Thailand). They reported bi-directional linkages of India with its major counterparts. Joshi12 studied the volatility spillover in Asian stock markets (Japan, Jakarta, India, China, Hong Kong, and Korea) by using GARCH model. They reported bidirectional co-movement among the stock markets. Furthermore, he finds that volatility spillover within the market is higher than volatility spillover across the markets. Sidek and Abdul-Rahman23 investigated the spillover effect of US sub-prime crisis on ASEAN-5 Stock returns (Malaysia, Philippines, Thailand, Singapore, and Indonesia). They found that the ASEAN stock markets returns decreased due to increase in volatility of US stock market returns. Most recently, Jebran and Iqbal11, 24 studied the Asian stock markets and found linkages among the markets.Several studies have been carried out to investigate the effect of financial crisis on equity market integrations and most of those concluded an increase in integration among markets in post financial crisis period. The 1987 European market crisis was investigated by studies like.25, 26, 27 The 1998 Russian financial crisis was studied by Diebold and Yilma28 and Patev et al.29 The effects of 1997 Asian financial crisis on stock market integrations was investigated by Arshanapalli et al,30 Jang and Sul,31 Li and Giles,5 Park and Song,32 and Patev et al.29 The effect of most recent 2007 subprime Asian financial crisis was investigated by studies like.1, 4, 5, 23 Though the literature is wide spread, there is still a need to investigate the emerging markets of Asia in the recent financial crisis period. This study aimed to investigate the integrations of emerging markets of Asian in 2007 financial crisis period. This study will therefore provide information about the integrations of emerging markets of Asia in pre and post 2007 financial crisis period.3. Methodology3.1. DataThis study aims to investigate the volatility spillover dynamics between the selected emerging Asian stock markets considering pre-crisis and post-crisis period. This study considered data from 2nd January, 2001 to 31st December, 2013. This study used daily data for analysis because by using daily data, we can capture more information than with weekly and monthly data.5, 11 The daily data consist of 5 trading days i.e. from Monday to Friday. The data is further divided into two sub-periods i.e. pre-crisis and post-crisis period. The pre-crisis period is from 2nd January, 2001 to 30th July, 2007. The post-crisis period is from 1st August, 2007 to 31st December, 2013. The reason for selection of this period is that the US subprime financial crisis period is covered in the selected period. In addition, this study will also explore that whether volatility spillover between the emerging Asian financial markets varies from normal to turbulent periods. This study considered more than 6 years data as post-crisis period because as pointed by He33 and Li and Giles5 changes in market correlation takes place on continuous basis and the correlation might not only be due to market crisis but there are many other events responsible for such correlations like major financial, economic and political events.The five emerging stock market indices of Asia that we considered are the following: Pakistan Stock Exchange (formerly Karachi Stock Exchange) 100 index for Pakistan; Shanghai Stock Exchange for China; S&P Bombay Stock Exchange for India; Hang Seng Stock Exchange for Hong Kong; and Colombo Stock Exchange for Sri Lanka.e The widely used stock market indices are selected for the five emerging economies. The daily data are obtained from Yahoofinance.com.3.2. MethodologyThis study aims to investigate the asymmetric volatility spillovers between financial markets. The asymmetric volatility spillover is aimed to be explored by using Exponential Generalized Autoregressive Conditional Heteroskedasticity (EGARCH) model. We investigate the asymmetric volatility spillovers because it is most widely argued that asymmetric (negative) shocks generate more volatility than symmetric (positive) shocks of the same magnitude. Before estimating the EGARCH model, the time series properties of the data is examined. The EGARCH model can be applied on the data which is stationary at levels. Furthermore another assumption of EGARCH model is that there should be ARCH effect in the data. In simple words, there should be autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity problems in the data. The aforementioned properties of data are examined before estimating EGARCH model.In the first step, we examine the stationarity of the data sets. There are different unit root tests to examine the stationarity of the data. The most widely used unit root tests are ADF34 test and PP35 test. These tests were followed to examine the stationarity of the data sets.In the second step, we investigate the ARCH effect in the data sets. In simple words, the data sets are examined for problems of autocorrelation and heteroskedasticity. This test is carried out by taking first lag of the returns of data sets and then applying the ARCH test on residuals. The significant ARCH effect can be confirmed when the probability of Chi-square in ARCH test is less than 5%.After confirmation of stationarity, and ARCH effects in the data sets, we estimate EGARCH model for exploring volatility spillover dynamics between the financial markets. Following Jan & Jebran9 and Jebran & Iqbal,11, 24 this study followed a two step procedure to examine the volatility spillover between the financial markets. In the first step, we generated volatility residuals using a GARCH model for each stock market data separately. In the second step, the generated volatility residuals of the stock market data sets were introduced to the variance equation of the EGARCH model and were used as proxy of shock emanating from one market to other market. The specification of extended EGARCH model is represented as follows.(1)ɛRt=ω0+ω1Rt−1+ψRt−1(stock indices)+ɛt(2)ɛɛlnht =γ0+γ1+|ɛt−1ht−1|+φɛt−1ht−1+γ2ht−1+δ(volatility residuals of stock indices)Equations (1), (2) represent the extended EGARCH model. Equations (1), (2) are mean and variance equation respectively. In equation (1), Rt represents the continuous compounding returns of the stock index dataset; ω0 is the constant; ω1 measures the impact of lagged return on the stock market index; the ψ measures the return spillover from the stock indices to the underlying stock market. In equation (2), ln ht represents the log of variance of the underlying variable; γ0 is constant level of volatility; γ1 measure the reaction of volatility to change in news; γ2 is function of volatility, it explains the consistence; φ represents the asymmetric effect of volatility. The parameter δ measures volatility spillover from the respective stock markets to the stock market under consideration. In equation (2), the “volatility residuals of stock indices” represents the volatility residuals for each stock market index data sets, which have been used as a proxy of volatility spillover.4. Conclusion and implicationsThis paper contributes to the literature of volatility spillover among Asian emerging economies in pre and post subprime financial crisis period. The pre-crisis period is considered from 2nd January, 2001 to 30th July, 2007. The post-crisis period is considered from 1st August, 2007 to 31st December, 2013. The asymmetric volatility spillover is captured using the extended EGARCH model. The results highlight certain interesting key findings. The results indicate that volatility spillover varies from normal to turbulent periods. The stock market becomes more integrated in a crisis situation. Similarly the volatility spillover between the stock markets increases and financial markets become more integrated during the crisis period. Furthermore we find that negative shocks generate more volatility than positive shocks of the same magnitude.This study has some important implications for investors, policy makers, and corporations. The integrations among financial markets (in terms of volatility spillover) suggest low potential diversification opportunities for investors. Investors may design their investments strategies by considering the integrations of different financial markets. Furthermore, the policy makers may consider integrations of financial markets as an important phenomenon because the subprime financial crisis spillover from one market to other market and it affected the financial performance of the markets.AcknowledgmentWe are thankful to the reviewers' for helpful comments.
