IntroductionIn mid-June 2015, stock markets in China had a three-week plunge that knocked off 30% Chinese shares. In the first week of 2016 after shares plunged by 7% on both Monday and Thursday, the circuit beakers shut down China's main market. The Shanghai Composite Index fell 18% in the first two weeks of 2016. The mix of investors in Chinese stock markets is different compared with most global markets. As brokerages have lapped up people appetite for borrowing money and stock market bets, more households have become exposed to the risk of a stock market correction. Investors and policymakers around the world are looking on with growing concern that turmoil in the stock markets will spill into China's real economy, the second-largest in the world and a huge engine of global growth.China's newly established electronic equity transfer system (New Third Board) is likely to gain the limelight from the stock exchanges. The National Equities and Exchange Quotations has attracted more than 740 start-ups since its official inauguration at the end of 2013. The China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) has given full play to market forces to foster the rapid growth of the New Third Board, and the low listing threshold has ushered in a flood of small firms into the New Third Board market. Recently, on March 18th, 2016, NEEQ launched the Transfer Intention Platform in order to improve market service, reduce trading cost, and promote the price discovery ability at the market level. The CSRC's new chairman also declared that they would stick to the previous directions of the New Third Board during the two sessions of 2016 in China.In this paper, we study the New Third Board market in China systematically. Various properties for cross sectional (daily and weekly) returns and volatility are calculated and interpreted, the return–volatility relation for the New Third Board market are analyzed comprehensively. The New Third Board market started in 2006 with only a few firms, and it grew slowly before 2014. The number of listed firms in 2009, 2010 and 2011 was 46, 62 and 87, respectively. It then grew to 200 by the end of 2012, 356 by the end of 2013 and 1572 by the end of 2014. Finally, in August 31 2015, there are 3359 listed firms, surpassing the combined 2800 companies listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock markets. Up to the end of 2012, only 43 listed companies had been traded. Then, the expansion started in 2013 and finally grew in a reasonable size in 2014. Therefore, the sample period used in this paper are constrained from January 2013 to August 2015.We specify both the daily simple returns and log-returns for all classification codes in the Management Industry Classification and all sectors in the Investment Industry Classification in addition to the whole market, and we repeat the computations for weekly returns. The mean and median simple returns for the whole New Third Board market are 40.29% and 5.97% respectively, indicating the positive returns overall. For the management industry classification, the Manufacturing sector and Information Transmission sector obtain the largest median returns 3.68% and 3.68%, while the first largest mean returns is 2.3661 for Transportation, Storage and Postal sector, indicating the existence of large outliers in the New Third Board market. The Manufacturing sector has the largest skewness and largest kurtosis in the Management Industry Classification. Similarly, we obtain the summary statistics of daily returns for the Investment Industry Classification. We have the overall 0.31% daily log-return mean, the 26% daily log-return standard deviation and left skewness with 2.06 and 22.65 kurtosis for the whole market. Robust analysis is performed further in order to check whether the outliers influence our results, by deleting observations satisfying returns higher than 50 or prices lower than 0.02. The behavior of median returns in each sector do not change much while significant changes are found for the mean returns, concluding that outliers do influence the performance of different sectors, and median returns seem to be a more reliable variable less influenced by the outliers. Analysis for the one year sub-period further confirms the results for the whole sample. Finally, we analyze the weekly returns for all sectors in the New Third Board market.The New Third Board market plays an important role in the Chinese stock markets by providing a convenient funding channel for high-growth enterprises and adding an important multilayer stock systems part in China. The problem for the New Third Board is its liquidity. Its average daily turnover is only about 500 million yuan and that once dropped to a low of 352 million yuan on July 15, 2015, according to South China Morning Post report. In terms of volatility, the New Third Board stocks are not capped by daily trading limits so some stocks may fluctuate 300 to 400 per cent a day. In Shanghai or Shenzhen Exchanges, shares have a 10 per cent daily limit move. The volatility might be the discouraged factor for long term investors in the New Third Board market.We further empirically study the daily close-to-close volatility, Parkinson volatility and high–low–close volatility for the two kinds of industry classifications of the New Third Board market. We focus on the daily volatility for three different time-lags. For the management Industry Classification, the Finance sector is the most volatile one, followed by the Mining sector in terms of Parkinson volatility and high–low–close volatility, while the Mining sector is more volatile than the Finance sector for the close-to-close volatility. The Education sector and the Real Estate sector have the lowest daily volatility. From the results for the Investment Industry Classification, the Finance sector again obtains the highest daily volatility, while the Real Estate sector has the lowest daily volatility. The statistics for daily volatility are robust when deleting the outliers of closing prices.Finally, we check the relation between stock returns and return volatility for the individual firms and each sector of the New Third Board market. French et al1 find several estimates of the relation between the expected risk premium and the predicted volatility of NYSE common stocks during the 1928–1984 period. Their estimations of volatility and expected risk premiums have fluctuated widely over the period between January 1928 and December 1984. Duffee2 finds that the contemporaneous relation between returns and volatility is strongly positive and the relation between returns and one-period-ahead volatility depends on the frequency over which these relations are measured for the New York Stock Exchanges. In our paper, under the related short sample from January 2013 to August 2015, we find that the New Third Board shows negative relation between the returns and the future changes of volatility for both the setups of Black3 and Christie.4 The contemporaneous relation between returns and volatility is positive, and the relation between returns and one-period-ahead volatility is much weaker with weekly frequency. These relations hold for the whole New Third Board market and most sectors divided according to the Management Industry Classification and Investment Industry Classification. These relations remain robust for those firms with at least 12 weeks returns or firms with at least 24 weeks returns, no matter how volatility is measured, by standard deviation or adjusted from first-order autocorrelation in returns. By considering the size effect, we find that the firm size is negatively correlated with λ0 (as in the expression ln(σt+1σt)=α0+λ0rt+εt+1,0, Duffee2 argues that the primary reason for λ0<0 is that a positive rt corresponds to an increase in σt) at weekly frequency for the New Third Board market, opposite with Duffee's2 findings that the positive correlations of size and λ0 at both the monthly and daily frequency for the American or New York Stock Exchanges in the period 1977–1991. Firm size is positively correlated with λ1, and negatively correlated with λ2, where ln(σt+i)=αi+1+λi+1+εt+i,i+1 for i = 0 and i = 1 and α0=α2−α1 and λ0=λ2−λ1. The negative correlation of λ0 with the firm size is a consequence of that the size effect in λ2 is weaker than the size effect in λ1 for the New Third Board, which is also opposite with Duffee's2 results for NYSE over 1977–1991.Jingu5 reports that China's New Third Board may play a key role in a capital market for venture companies in China due to China's goal of becoming an “innovative nation” by 2020. Liang and Wei6 find that the innovation in volatility of the global market portfolio is a systematic pricing risk factor across locally diversified market, i.e., local market volatility risk in a country drives that country's aggregate stock market return in the global setting portfolios after controlling for global market, value, and size factors. Li et al7 give a comprehensive and systematic knowledge framework of China's New Third Board Market for those enterprise and individuals interested in it. Evans and McMilan8 examine the nature of equity ownership of state-owned enterprises (SOEs) for over 2000 listed firms in China, with literature review of research on Chinese Stock Markets, rather than on the New Third Board market. There is no literature of study for the New Third Board market in China, up to our knowledge. Our paper is the first to study the New Third Board market in China on returns, volatility and their relations.The remainder this paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we describe the New Third Board and data with basic trading information. Returns from daily base and weekly base for the New Third Board are presented in Section 3 for each sector in both Classifications. Section 4 devotes to analyze various volatility measures for sectors in the New Third Board market. We draw the relation between the return and the volatility for each sector in the New Third Board market in Section 5. Conclusions are given in the last Section 6.2. Data2.1. Big eventsKnown as China's share transfer system for the small and medium-sized companies, the New Third Board market as China's OTC (over-the-counter) market is different from the main board markets in both Shanghai and Shenzheng Exchanges. It is also the unique OTC market supervised by China Securities Regulatory Commission (CSRC) which is designed specifically for non-public firms to seek capitals and investors to trade equities in China.On January 17th, 2006, the unlisted companies of Zhongguancun Science and Technology Garden initiated negotiable quotation in Agency Share Transfer System, the New Third Board was set up, only covering companies registered in Zhongguancun Science Park at Beijing, China. In early 2011, the related regulations and rules for the New Third Board market were completed. On August 3rd, 2012, Pilot Project of Private Company Share Transfer was expanded to Shanghai Zhangjiang High-technology Garden, Wuhan Donghu Innovative technology Garden and Tianjin Binhai High-tech Zone. On September 20th, 2012, the National Equities Exchange and Quotations System Co., Ltd. (NEEQ) claimed its role for the New Third Board market as the operation management institution. The functions of NEEQ include: establishment, maintenance and improvement of stock-exchange-related technology system and facilities; development and modification of national equities exchange system business rules; acceptance and examination of the applications of stock listing and other related business and the arrangement of the listing of qualified companies; organization and supervision of stock exchange and the related activities; supervision of the participants (lead securities firms) of the national equities exchange system; supervision of listed companies and other information disclosure agents; management and announcement of related information of national equities exchange system, and other functions as approved by China Securities Regulatory Commission (see Li et al7).The New Third Board market plays an important role in providing a convenient funding channel for high-growth enterprises in early stage, and adds an important part of multi-layer stock systems in China. On June 19th, 2013, the State Council announced that it would expand the New Third Board to include all the small and medium-sized enterprises ventures across the whole country. On December 14th, 2013, the New Third Board market changed its policies to eliminate the requirement that only companies in national high-tech zones were allowed to be included, and all firms accordant with trading requirements can apply to be listed. This marks that the OTC market started to cover the country nationwide. On December 30th, 2013, NEEQ announced the approval requirement. On January 24th, 2014, the first batch of firms outside national high-tech zones was traded in NEEQ. At this moment, the total number of listed firms on NEEQ reached 621. On August 25th, 2014, NEEQ launched the market making transfer transaction mechanism for trading shares on the New Third Board. On March 19th, 2015, NEEQ further announced the launch of its first two equity indexes, Third Board Component Index and Third Board Market Making Index with index code being 899001 and 899002, respectively. The two indices aim to reflect the pricing fluctuation and the performance of the whole New Third Board market and the market making stocks, respectively. The indices are released after the market closes and adjusted every quarter.2.2. Listed companiesThe trading information including daily prices and trading volume for all firm of the New Third Board are downloaded from WIND database, the basic summary of this market was obtained from the NEEQ website and the Choice Financial Terminal of Eastern Wealth Network (CFTEWN). CFTEWN has two versions for the summary of the listed companies for New Third Board market.dThe companies listed on the New Third Board market are usually characterized by high innovation, high growth or high technology. The requirement for listing on the New Third Board should satisfy that the company must be established according to the law and have subsisted for at least two years; it must have a well-defined business and the ability to continue as an ongoing business; it must have a sound corporate governance mechanism and carry out its operations in a lawful and regulated manner; it must have well-defined shareholding and the issue and transfer of its shares must be legal and compliant; and it must be recommended by a securities trader and be subjected to its ongoing supervision. It has been extremely small since its inception in 2006.e The number of listed companies in the New Third Board market surpasses the combined 2800 companies listed on the Shanghai and Shenzhen stock markets. In addition, the listed companies are distributed in 30 provinces and autonomous regions of China, among which Beijing leads followed by Jiangsu province.Based on Articles of NEEQ, corporators with registered capital of at least 5 million yuan, partnership enterprises with the total paid-in capital of at least 5 million yuan and qualified personal investors can apply for participation in the public equities exchange of listed companies in the New Third Board market. Personal investors are subject to (1) already own securities assets at least 5 million yuan in the previous trading day and (2) should have more than two years of investment experience in securities or a professional background related to accounting, investment and finance.2.3. Daily returnsWe further train the raw data by deleting those zero prices for return calculations. Daily returns are calculated by using the closing prices on the trading dates, and each return is obtained by comparing the price with the previous nonzero effective price. Moreover, we further delete those zero returns with the same prices on some consecutive dates. Finally, 1311 firms have been traded during our sample period from January 2013 to August 2015, with a total of 69,620 effective nonzero close-to-close return observations.In this section, we calculate daily returns for each individual stock by evaluating the simple return Rt and the continuously compounded return (log-return) rt, with rt=ln(1+Rt).The daily simple return Rt of a stock on date t is given by(3.1)Rt=Pt−Pt−1Pt−1=PtPt−1−1where Pt is the closing price on day t.The daily continuously compounded return (log-return) rt of a stock on day t is given by(3.2)rt=ln(1+Rt)=lnPtPt−1=pt−pt−1where pt = ln Pt is the logarithm of the closing price Pt on day t.With both the Investment Industry and Management Industry classifications, we calculate the arithmetic average of daily returns within each sector for all the trading days, and use them to represent the sector returns. VolatilityEven though the New Third Board has a short history, we still measure the historical volatility for the New Third Board market. Volatility evaluated from historical data reflects the past price movements of the underlying asset. There are various methods to measure historical volatility based on the open, high, low and closing prices of the underlying stock. In this section, we adopt three kinds of methods to calculate the historical volatility for each sector of the New Third Board market.(i)Close to Close Historical Volatility (HV)The first volatility is the standard deviation of stock returns over a fixed number of days. The return–volatility relationIn this section, we turn our attention to the relation between stock returns and return volatility for the individual firms and each sector of the New Third Board market.Black1 used 30 stocks from the Dow Jones Industrials to calculate monthly stock return volatility over the period 1962–1975 by summing squared daily returns and taking the square root of the result. For each stock i, the relation between stock returns and volatility is estimated as(5.1)σi,t+1−σi,tσi,t=α0+λ0ri,t+εi,t+1,where ri,t is the ith firm's stock return at the end of period t, σi,t is the estimate of stock return volatility at the end of period t.Christie4 constructed quarterly estimates of stock return volatility over the period 1962–1978 for about 379 firms by the following regression(5.2)lnσt+1−lnσt=α0+λ0rt+εt+1,0.Black3 found that λ0 was always negative and usually less than −1, and Christie4 estimated (5.2) for each firm and found a mean λ0 of −0.23.Duffee2 proposes a new interpretation for the negative relation between stock returns and changes in future return volatility at the firm level, by splitting (5.2) into two regressions(5.3)lnσt+1=α2+λ2rt+εt+1,2;lnσt=α1+λ1rt+εt,1with α0=α2−α1,λ0=λ2−λ1.Duffee2 estimates (5.3) based on stock returns of almost 2500 firms traded on the American or New York Stock Exchanges from 1977 to 1991, and found that λ1 is strongly positive and the sign of λ2 depends on the frequency over which these relations are estimated, positive at daily frequency and negative at monthly frequency. In both cases, λ1 always exceeds λ2, He reconfirmed Cheung and Ng's10 result that λ0 and firm size are strongly positively correlated, but find that this positive correlation is driven by the negative correlation between firm size and λ1.Instead of using monthly or daily returns and volatility, we check the performance of the New Third Board market by adopting weekly data. ConclusionIn this paper, we specify the daily simple return and log-return for both Management Industry Classification and Investment Industry Classification of the New Third Board market from January 2013 to August 2015. The mean and median simple returns for the whole New Third Board market are 40.29% and 5.97% respectively, indicating the positive returns overall. For the Management Industry classification, the Manufacturing sector and the Information Transmission sector obtain the largest median returns 0.0368 and 0.0368, while the largest mean returns 2.3661 is for the Transportation, Storage and Postal sector, indicating the existence of large outliers in the New Third Board market. The Manufacturing sector has the largest skewness and largest kurtosis in the Management Industry Classification. Similarly, we obtain the summary statistics of daily returns for the Investment Industry Classification. We have the overall 0.31% daily log-return mean, the 26% daily log-return standard deviation and left skewness with 2.06 and 22.65 kurtosis for the whole market.Robust analysis is performed further to check whether the outliers influence our results, by deleting observations satisfying returns higher than 50 or prices lower than 0.02 (outliers do influence the performance of different sectors, and median returns seem to be a more reliable variable less influenced by the outliers.). The behavior of median returns in each sector do not change much, while significant changes are found for the mean returns. Analysis for the one year sub-period further confirms the results for the whole sample. Finally, we also analyze the weekly returns for all sectors in the New Third Board market. We compute the overall 315.44% weekly simple return mean, the 18.39 weekly simple return standard deviation and right skewness with 9.08 and 100.39 kurtosis for both the Management Industry Classification and the Investment Industry Classification.The paper's estimates of various volatility for the New Third Board market in China show that the daily close-to-close volatility, the daily Parkinson volatility and the daily high–low–close volatility fluctuated overall around 19% and 28% during the period we examined in this paper. For the Management Industry Classification, the Finance sector is the most volatile with mean 121% in 10 days block, and 134% in 20 days block and 188% in 60 days block in terms of the Parkinson's historical volatility. That is followed by the Mining sector. In fact we find the evidence that the Finance sector is the most volatile sector in the high–low–close historical volatility measure as well as in the Parkinson's historical volatility measure, while the Mining sector is more volatile then Finance sector for the close-to-close volatility. The Education sector and the Real Estate sector have the lowest daily volatility. From the results for the Investment Industry Classification, the Finance sector again obtains the highest daily volatility, while the Real Estate sector has the lowest daily volatility. The statistics for daily volatility are robust when deleting the outliers of closing prices. The statistics for daily volatility are robust when deleting the outliers of closing prices by eliminating the observations with returns over 50 or stock prices lower than 0.02. For the time-varying volatility, we focus on the daily volatility for three different time-lags.We find evidence of negative relation between the returns and the future changes of volatility for both setups in Black3 and in Christie4 for the New Third Board market in China. By considering the size effect, we find that the firm size is negatively correlated with λ0 at weekly frequency for the New Third Board market, the opposite with Duffee's2 findings that the positive correlations of size and λ0 at both the monthly and daily frequency for the American or New York Stock Exchanges in the period 1977–1991. Firm size is positive correlated with λ1, and negative correlated with λ2. The negative correlation of λ0 with size is a consequence of the size effect in λ2 being stronger than the size effect in λ1 for the New Third Board, which is also opposite with Duffee's2 results for NYSE over 1977–1991.
