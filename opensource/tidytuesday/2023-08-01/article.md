The [United States of America](/wiki/United_States "United States") is a [federal republic](/wiki/Federal_republic "Federal republic")[\[1]](#cite_note-1) consisting of 50 [states](/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state"), a [federal district](/wiki/Capital_districts_and_territories#United_States "Capital districts and territories") ([Washington, D.C.](/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), the [capital city](/wiki/Capital_city "Capital city") of the United States), five major [territories](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States "Territories of the United States"), and various [minor islands](/wiki/United_States_Minor_Outlying_Islands "United States Minor Outlying Islands").[\[2]](#cite_note-2)[\[3]](#cite_note-3) Both the states and the United States as a whole are each sovereign jurisdictions.[\[4]](#cite_note-Radan,_2007,_p._12-4) The [Tenth Amendment to the United States Constitution](/wiki/Tenth_Amendment_to_the_United_States_Constitution "Tenth Amendment to the United States Constitution") allows states to exercise all powers of government not delegated to the federal government. Each state has its own [constitution](/wiki/State_constitution_(United_States) "State constitution (United States)") and government, and all states and their residents are represented in the federal [Congress](/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress"), a [bicameral](/wiki/Bicameral "Bicameral") legislature consisting of the [Senate](/wiki/United_States_Senate "United States Senate") and the [House of Representatives](/wiki/United_States_House_of_Representatives "United States House of Representatives"). Each state elects two senators, while representatives are [distributed](/wiki/United_States_congressional_apportionment "United States congressional apportionment") among the states in [proportion](/wiki/Apportionment_(politics) "Apportionment (politics)") to the most recent [constitutionally](/wiki/United_States_Constitution "United States Constitution") mandated decennial [census](/wiki/United_States_census "United States census").[\[5]](#cite_note-5) Additionally, each state is entitled to select a number of electors to vote in the [Electoral College](/wiki/United_States_Electoral_College "United States Electoral College"), the body that elects the [president of the United States](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States"), equal to the total of representatives and senators in Congress from that state.[\[6]](#cite_note-6) The federal district does not have representatives in the Senate, but has a non\-voting [delegate](/wiki/Delegate_(United_States_Congress) "Delegate (United States Congress)") in the House, and it is also entitled to electors in the Electoral College. Congress can [admit](/wiki/Admission_to_the_Union "Admission to the Union") more states, but it cannot create a new state from territory of an existing state or merge two or more states into one without the consent of all states involved, and each new state is admitted on an [equal footing](/wiki/Equal_footing "Equal footing") with the existing states.[\[7]](#cite_note-7)


The United States has control over fourteen territories. Five of them ([American Samoa](/wiki/American_Samoa "American Samoa"), [Guam](/wiki/Guam "Guam"), the [Northern Mariana Islands](/wiki/Northern_Mariana_Islands "Northern Mariana Islands"), [Puerto Rico](/wiki/Puerto_Rico "Puerto Rico"), and the [United States Virgin Islands](/wiki/United_States_Virgin_Islands "United States Virgin Islands")) have a permanent, nonmilitary population, while nine of them (the United States Minor Outlying Islands) do not. With the exception of [Navassa Island](/wiki/Navassa_Island "Navassa Island"), Puerto Rico, and the U.S. Virgin Islands, which are located in the [Caribbean](/wiki/Caribbean "Caribbean"), all territories are located in the [Pacific Ocean](/wiki/Pacific_Ocean "Pacific Ocean"). One territory, [Palmyra Atoll](/wiki/Palmyra_Atoll "Palmyra Atoll"), is considered to be [incorporated](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States#Incorporated_and_unincorporated_territories "Territories of the United States"), meaning the full body of the Constitution has been applied to it; the other territories are [unincorporated](/wiki/Unincorporated_territories_of_the_United_States "Unincorporated territories of the United States"), meaning the Constitution does not fully apply to them. Ten territories (the Minor Outlying Islands and American Samoa) are considered to be [unorganized](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States#Organized_vs._unorganized_territories "Territories of the United States"), meaning they have not had an [organic act](/wiki/Organic_act "Organic act") enacted by Congress; the four other territories are [organized](/wiki/Territories_of_the_United_States#Organized_vs._unorganized_territories "Territories of the United States"), meaning an organic act has been enacted by Congress. The five inhabited territories each have limited autonomy in addition to having territorial legislatures and governors, but residents cannot vote in federal elections, although all are represented by non\-voting delegates in the House.


The largest state by population is [California](/wiki/California "California"), with a population of 39,538,223 people, while the smallest is [Wyoming](/wiki/Wyoming "Wyoming"), with a population of 576,851 people; the federal district has a larger population (689,545\) than both Wyoming and [Vermont](/wiki/Vermont "Vermont"). The largest state by area is [Alaska](/wiki/Alaska "Alaska"), encompassing 665,384 square miles (1,723,340 km2), while the smallest is [Rhode Island](/wiki/Rhode_Island "Rhode Island"), encompassing 1,545 square miles (4,000 km2). The most recent states to be admitted, Alaska and [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii"), were admitted in 1959\. The largest territory by population is Puerto Rico, with a population of 3,285,874 people (larger than 21 states), while the smallest is the Northern Mariana Islands, with a population of 47,329 people. Puerto Rico is the largest territory by area, encompassing 5,325 square miles (13,790 km2); the smallest territory, [Kingman Reef](/wiki/Kingman_Reef "Kingman Reef"), encompasses only 0\.005 square miles (0\.013 km2), or a little larger than 3 acres.

1. **[^](#cite_ref-10)** The original 13 states became [sovereign](/wiki/Sovereignty "Sovereignty") in July 1776 upon agreeing to the [United States Declaration of Independence](/wiki/United_States_Declaration_of_Independence "United States Declaration of Independence"), and each joined the first Union of states between 1777 and 1781, upon ratifying the [Articles of Confederation](/wiki/Articles_of_Confederation "Articles of Confederation").[\[9]](#cite_note-9) These states are presented in the order in which each ratified the 1787 Constitution, thus joining the present federal Union of states. Subsequent states are listed in the order of their admission to the Union, and the date given is the official establishment date set by [Act of Congress](/wiki/Act_of_Congress "Act of Congress"). *For further details, see [List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union](/wiki/List_of_U.S._states_by_date_of_admission_to_the_Union "List of U.S. states by date of admission to the Union")*
