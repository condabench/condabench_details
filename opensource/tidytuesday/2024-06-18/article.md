**Federal holidays in the United States** are 11 [calendar dates](/wiki/Calendar_date "Calendar date") designated by the [U.S. federal government](/wiki/Federal_government_of_the_United_States "Federal government of the United States") as holidays. On these days non\-essential U.S. federal government offices are closed and federal employees are paid for the day off.[\[1]](#cite_note-commerce/holidays-1)


Federal holidays are designated by the [United States Congress](/wiki/United_States_Congress "United States Congress") in Title V of the [United States Code](/wiki/United_States_Code "United States Code") ([5 U.S.C.](/wiki/Title_5_of_the_United_States_Code "Title 5 of the United States Code") [§ 6103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/5/6103)).[\[2]](#cite_note-cornell-2) Congress only has authority to create holidays for federal institutions (including federally\-owned properties), employees, and the [District of Columbia](/wiki/District_of_Columbia "District of Columbia"). As a general rule of courtesy, custom, and sometimes regulation, other institutions, such as banks, businesses, schools, and the [financial markets](/wiki/Financial_markets "Financial markets"), may be closed on federal holidays. In various parts of the country, [state](/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") and city holidays may be observed concurrently with federal holidays.




History
-------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
The history of national holidays in the United States dates back to June 28, 1870, when Congress created national holidays "to correspond with similar laws of States around the District...and...in every State of the Union."[\[3]](#cite_note-3) Although at first applicable only to federal employees in the [District of Columbia](/wiki/Washington,_D.C. "Washington, D.C."), Congress extended coverage in 1885 to all federal employees.


The original four holidays in 1870 were:



* [New Year's Day](/wiki/New_Year%27s_Day "New Year's Day")
* [Independence Day](/wiki/Independence_Day_(United_States) "Independence Day (United States)")
* [Thanksgiving Day](/wiki/Thanksgiving_Day_(United_States) "Thanksgiving Day (United States)")
* [Christmas Day](/wiki/Christmas_Day "Christmas Day")


[George Washington's Birthday](/wiki/George_Washington%27s_Birthday "George Washington's Birthday") became a federal holiday in 1879\. In 1888 and 1894, respectively, Decoration Day (now [Memorial Day](/wiki/Memorial_Day_(United_States) "Memorial Day (United States)")) and [Labor Day](/wiki/Labor_Day "Labor Day") were created. Armistice Day was established in 1938 to honor the end of [World War I](/wiki/World_War_I "World War I"), and the scope of the holiday was expanded to honor Americans who fought in [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II "World War II") and the [Korean War](/wiki/Korean_War "Korean War") when it was renamed [Veterans Day](/wiki/Veterans_Day "Veterans Day") in 1954\.


In 1968, the [Uniform Monday Holiday Act](/wiki/Uniform_Monday_Holiday_Act "Uniform Monday Holiday Act") gave several holidays "floating" dates so that they always fall on a Monday, and also established [Columbus Day](/wiki/Columbus_Day "Columbus Day").


In 1983, President [Ronald Reagan](/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan") signed the bill that created Dr. [Martin Luther King Jr. Day](/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr._Day "Martin Luther King Jr. Day"). It was first observed three years later, although some states resisted making it a state holiday. It was finally celebrated both nationally and by each of the states in 2000\.[\[4]](#cite_note-4) Dr. King's birthday is January 15th.


On June 17, 2021, President [Joe Biden](/wiki/Joe_Biden "Joe Biden") signed the law that made June 19th a federal holiday. Officially designated as "[Juneteenth National Independence Day](/wiki/Juneteenth_National_Independence_Day "Juneteenth National Independence Day")", Juneteenth commemorates the belated June 19, 1865 announcement that enslaved people in the Confederate states had been freed by President Abraham Lincoln as of January 1, 1863\.[\[5]](#cite_note-5)[\[6]](#cite_note-6)[\[7]](#cite_note-7)[\[8]](#cite_note-8) President Abraham Lincoln issued the Emancipation Proclamation on January 1, 1863 which declared "that all persons held as slaves" within the rebellious states "are, and henceforward shall be free". A National Park Service fact sheet explains that "the word “Juneteenth” is a Black English contraction, or portmanteau, of the month “June” and the date “Nineteenth”. Juneteenth celebrates the date of June 19, 1865, when enslaved people of African descent located in Galveston, Texas, finally learned of their freedom from the slavery system in the United States."[\[9]](#cite_note-9) President Biden's annual proclamation in 2024 began, "On June 19, 1865, freedom finally came for the 250,000 enslaved people of Texas. That day, which would become known as Juneteenth, the Army arrived to enforce what had already been the law of the land for two and a half years — the Emancipation Proclamation".[\[10]](#cite_note-10)


On October 3, 1789, at the request of the U.S. Congress, President George Washington issued a Thanksgiving proclamation designating November 26, 1789 as a day of "public thanksgiving and prayer" for the "People of the United States”.[\[11]](#cite_note-11)[\[12]](#cite_note-12) Thanksgiving has been proclaimed in the U.S. for various months and days of the week, including March, August, September, November, December, and on Sundays.[\[13]](#cite_note-13)[\[14]](#cite_note-14)


Celebrated on December 25th around the world, Christmas is the day that Christians remember the birth and [incarnation](/wiki/Incarnation_(Christianity) "Incarnation (Christianity)") of [Jesus Christ](/wiki/Jesus "Jesus"). Christmas has been celebrated for over 2,000 years. In the United States, Christmas Day as a federal or [public holiday](/wiki/Public_holidays_in_the_United_States "Public holidays in the United States") is sometimes objected to by various non\-Christians,[\[15]](#cite_note-15)[\[16]](#cite_note-16)[\[17]](#cite_note-17) usually due to its ties with [Christianity](/wiki/Christianity "Christianity"). In December 1999, the Western Division of the [United States District Court for the Southern District of Ohio](/wiki/United_States_District_Court_for_the_Southern_District_of_Ohio "United States District Court for the Southern District of Ohio"), in the case *Ganulin v. United States*, denied the charge that Christmas Day's federal status violated the [Establishment Clause](/wiki/Establishment_Clause "Establishment Clause") of the [Constitution](/wiki/Constitution_of_the_United_States "Constitution of the United States"), ruling that "the Christmas holiday has become largely secularized", and that "by giving federal employees a paid vacation day on Christmas, the government is doing no more than recognizing the cultural significance of the holiday".[\[18]](#cite_note-18)[\[19]](#cite_note-19)



List of federal holidays
------------------------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: List of federal holidays")]
Most of the 11[\[20]](#cite_note-hr.commerce.gov-20) U.S. federal holidays are also [state](/wiki/U.S._state "U.S. state") holidays. Five of the "floating" date holidays always fall on a Monday, the remaining floating holiday, Thanksgiving, is always on a Thursday. The rest are on fixed dates. A fixed date holiday that falls on a [weekend](/wiki/Weekend "Weekend") (Saturday and Sunday) is usually observed for federal employees on the closest [weekday](/wiki/Weekday "Weekday"): a holiday falling on a Saturday is observed on the preceding Friday, while a holiday falling on a Sunday is observed on the succeeding Monday.[\[21]](#cite_note-opm-21) The official names come from the statute that defines holidays for federal employees.

[Inauguration Day](/wiki/United_States_presidential_inauguration "United States presidential inauguration"), held on January 20 every four years following a quadrennial [presidential election](/wiki/United_States_presidential_election "United States presidential election"), is considered a paid holiday for federal employees in the [Washington, D.C., area](/wiki/Washington_metropolitan_area "Washington metropolitan area") by the [Office of Personnel Management](/wiki/United_States_Office_of_Personnel_Management "United States Office of Personnel Management"). It is not considered a federal holiday in the United States equivalent to the eleven holidays mentioned above.[\[33]](#cite_note-33)


Although many states recognize most or all federal holidays as state holidays, the federal government cannot enact laws to compel them to do so. States can recognize other days as state holidays that are not federal holidays. For example, the State of Texas recognizes all federal holidays except Columbus Day, and recognizes the Friday after Thanksgiving, Christmas Eve, and the day after Christmas as state holidays.[\[34]](#cite_note-texas-34)


Texas does not follow the federal rule of closing either the Friday before, if a holiday falls on a Saturday, or the Monday after if a holiday falls on a Sunday. Offices are open on those Fridays or Mondays. Texas has "partial staffing holidays", such as [March 2](/wiki/March_2 "March 2"), which is [Texas Independence Day](/wiki/Texas_Independence_Day "Texas Independence Day"), and "optional holidays", such as [Good Friday](/wiki/Good_Friday "Good Friday").[\[34]](#cite_note-texas-34)


Private employers are not required to observe federal or state holidays, the key exception being federally\-chartered banks. Some private employers, often by a union contract, pay a differential such as [time\-and\-a\-half](/wiki/Time-and-a-half "Time-and-a-half") or double\-time to employees who work on some federal holidays. Employees not specifically covered by a union contract, might only receive their standard pay for working on a federal holiday, depending on the company policy.



Legal holidays due to presidential proclamation
-----------------------------------------------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Legal holidays due to presidential proclamation")]
Main article: [List of observances in the United States by presidential proclamation](/wiki/List_of_observances_in_the_United_States_by_presidential_proclamation "List of observances in the United States by presidential proclamation")
Federal law also provides for the declaration of other public holidays by the [President of the United States](/wiki/President_of_the_United_States "President of the United States"). Generally the president will provide a reasoning behind the elevation of the day, and call on the people of the United States to observe the day "with appropriate ceremonies and activities."


Examples of presidentially declared holidays were the days of the funerals for former Presidents [Ronald Reagan](/wiki/Ronald_Reagan "Ronald Reagan"), [George H. W. Bush](/wiki/George_H._W._Bush "George H. W. Bush"), and [Gerald Ford](/wiki/Gerald_Ford "Gerald Ford"); federal government offices were closed and employees given a paid holiday.


In addition, occasionally the President will grant [Christmas Eve](/wiki/Christmas_Eve "Christmas Eve") as a holiday or partial holiday (the latter generally being 4 hours for full\-time employees).



Proposed federal holidays
-------------------------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Proposed federal holidays")]
Many federal holidays have been proposed. As the U.S. federal government is a large employer, the holidays are expensive. If a holiday is controversial, opposition will generally prevent bills enacting them from passing. For example, [Martin Luther King Jr. Day](/wiki/Martin_Luther_King_Jr._Day "Martin Luther King Jr. Day"), marking King's birthday, took much effort to pass[\[35]](#cite_note-35) and for all states to recognize it. It was not until 2000 that this holiday was officially observed in all 50 states.[\[36]](#cite_note-36)

Controversy
-----------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=Federal_holidays_in_the_United_States&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Controversy")]

Some Native American groups protest the observance of [Columbus Day](/wiki/Columbus_Day "Columbus Day"), mainly due to the controversy of Columbus' arrival to the Americas.[\[47]](#cite_note-47)[\[48]](#cite_note-48)


[Alaska](/wiki/Alaska "Alaska"), [Colorado](/wiki/Colorado "Colorado"),[\[49]](#cite_note-49) [Florida](/wiki/Florida "Florida"), [Hawaii](/wiki/Hawaii "Hawaii"), [Iowa](/wiki/Iowa "Iowa"),[\[50]](#cite_note-50) [Louisiana](/wiki/Louisiana "Louisiana"),[\[51]](#cite_note-51) [Maine](/wiki/Maine "Maine"), [Michigan](/wiki/Michigan "Michigan"),[\[52]](#cite_note-52) [Minnesota](/wiki/Minnesota "Minnesota"),[\[53]](#cite_note-53) [New Mexico](/wiki/New_Mexico "New Mexico"), [Nevada](/wiki/Nevada "Nevada"), [North Carolina](/wiki/North_Carolina "North Carolina"),[\[54]](#cite_note-54) [Oregon](/wiki/Oregon "Oregon"), [South Dakota](/wiki/South_Dakota "South Dakota"), [Vermont](/wiki/Vermont "Vermont"),[\[55]](#cite_note-55) [Washington](/wiki/Washington_(state) "Washington (state)"), and [Wisconsin](/wiki/Wisconsin "Wisconsin") do not recognize Columbus Day. Hawaii and South Dakota mark the day with an alternative holiday or observance. South Dakota is the only state to recognize [Native American Day](/wiki/Native_American_Day "Native American Day") as an official state holiday.
