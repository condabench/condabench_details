Large international exhibition






Poster advertising the [Brussels International Exposition](/wiki/Brussels_International_Exposition_(1897) "Brussels International Exposition (1897)") in 1897


A **world's fair**, also known as a **universal exhibition** or an **expo**, is a large global exhibition designed to showcase the achievements of nations.[\[1]](#cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-1) These exhibitions vary in character and are held in different parts of the world at a specific site for a period of time, typically between three and six months.[\[1]](#cite_note-Encyclopædia_Britannica-1)


The term "world's fair" is commonly used in the United States,[\[2]](#cite_note-2) while the French term, *Exposition universelle* ("universal exhibition"[\[3]](#cite_note-3)) is used in most of Europe and Asia; other terms include **World Expo** or **Specialised Expo**, with the word expo used for various types of exhibitions since at least 1958\.


Since the adoption of the 1928 Convention Relating to International Exhibitions, the Paris\-based [Bureau International des Expositions](/wiki/Bureau_International_des_Expositions "Bureau International des Expositions") has served as an international sanctioning body for international exhibitions; four types of international exhibition are organised under its auspices: World Expos, Specialised Expos, Horticultural Expos (regulated by the [International Association of Horticultural Producers](/wiki/AIPH "AIPH")), and the [Milan Triennial](/wiki/Milan_Triennial "Milan Triennial").


[Astana](/wiki/Astana "Astana"), [Kazakhstan](/wiki/Kazakhstan "Kazakhstan"), held the most recent Specialised Expo [in 2017](/wiki/Expo_2017 "Expo 2017") while [Dubai](/wiki/Dubai "Dubai"), [United Arab Emirates](/wiki/United_Arab_Emirates "United Arab Emirates"), hosted World Expo [2020](/wiki/Expo_2020 "Expo 2020") (which was postponed to 2021 due to the [COVID\-19 pandemic](/wiki/COVID-19_pandemic "COVID-19 pandemic")) and [Doha](/wiki/Doha "Doha"), [Qatar](/wiki/Qatar "Qatar") hosted Horticultural Expo [in 2023](/wiki/International_Horticultural_Expo_2023_Doha_Qatar "International Horticultural Expo 2023 Doha Qatar").[\[4]](#cite_note-4)




History
-------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: History")]
Further information: [List of world expositions](/wiki/List_of_world_expositions "List of world expositions") and [List of world's fairs](/wiki/List_of_world%27s_fairs "List of world's fairs")
In 1791, [Prague](/wiki/Prague "Prague") organized the first World's Fair in [Bohemia](/wiki/Bohemia "Bohemia") (modern\-day [Czech Republic](/wiki/Czech_Republic "Czech Republic")).[\[5]](#cite_note-5)[\[6]](#cite_note-6)[\[7]](#cite_note-7) The first industrial exhibition was on the occasion of the coronation of [Leopold II](/wiki/Leopold_II,_Holy_Roman_Emperor "Leopold II, Holy Roman Emperor") as king of [Bohemia](/wiki/Bohemia "Bohemia"). The exhibition was held in the [Clementinum](/wiki/Clementinum "Clementinum"), and celebrated the considerable sophistication of manufacturing methods in the [Czech lands](/wiki/Czech_lands "Czech lands") during that time period.[\[8]](#cite_note-8) France had a [tradition of national exhibitions](/wiki/Exposition_des_produits_de_l%27industrie_fran%C3%A7aise "Exposition des produits de l'industrie française"), which culminated with the [French Industrial Exposition of 1844](/wiki/French_Industrial_Exposition_of_1844 "French Industrial Exposition of 1844") held in [Paris](/wiki/Paris "Paris"). This fair was followed by other national exhibitions in Europe. 





Interior of [the Crystal Palace](/wiki/The_Crystal_Palace "The Crystal Palace") at the [Great Exhibition](/wiki/Great_Exhibition "Great Exhibition") in [Hyde Park, London](/wiki/Hyde_Park,_London "Hyde Park, London"), in 1851


In 1851, the "Great Exhibition of the Works of Industry of All Nations" was held in [the Crystal Palace](/wiki/The_Crystal_Palace "The Crystal Palace") in [Hyde Park, London](/wiki/Hyde_Park,_London "Hyde Park, London"), United Kingdom. The [Great Exhibition](/wiki/Great_Exhibition "Great Exhibition"), as it is often called, was an idea of Prince [Albert](/wiki/Albert,_Prince_Consort "Albert, Prince Consort"), Queen [Victoria](/wiki/Queen_Victoria "Queen Victoria")'s husband, and is usually considered to be the first international exhibition of manufactured products.[\[9]](#cite_note-Gazette-9) It influenced the development of several aspects of society, including art\-and\-design education, international trade and relations, and tourism.[\[10]](#cite_note-10) This expo was the precedent for the many international exhibitions, later called *World Expos*, that have continued to be held to the present time.[\[9]](#cite_note-Gazette-9)


The character of world fairs, or expositions, has evolved since the first one in 1851\. Three eras can be distinguished: the era of industrialization, the era of cultural exchange, and the era of nation branding.[\[11]](#cite_note-11)



### Industrialization (1851–1938\)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Industrialization (1851–1938)")]


The Yerkes Great refractor telescope mounted at the 1893 World's Fair in Chicago


The first era, the era of "industrialization", roughly covered the years from 1850 to 1938\. In these years, world expositions were largely focused on trade and displayed technological advances and inventions. World expositions were platforms for state\-of\-the\-art science and technology from around the world. The world expositions of [1851 London](/wiki/Great_Exhibition "Great Exhibition"), [1853 New York](/wiki/Exhibition_of_the_Industry_of_All_Nations "Exhibition of the Industry of All Nations"), [1862 London](/wiki/1862_International_Exhibition "1862 International Exhibition"), [1876 Philadelphia](/wiki/Centennial_Exposition "Centennial Exposition"), [Paris 1878](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1878) "Exposition Universelle (1878)"), [1888 Barcelona](/wiki/1888_Barcelona_Universal_Exposition "1888 Barcelona Universal Exposition"), [1889 Paris](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1889) "Exposition Universelle (1889)"), [1891 Prague](/wiki/General_Land_Centennial_Exhibition_(1891) "General Land Centennial Exhibition (1891)"), [1893 Chicago](/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition "World's Columbian Exposition"), [1897 Brussels](/wiki/Brussels_International_Exposition_(1897) "Brussels International Exposition (1897)"), [1900 Paris](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1900) "Exposition Universelle (1900)"), [1904 St. Louis](/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase_Exposition "Louisiana Purchase Exposition"), [1915 San Francisco](/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93Pacific_International_Exposition "Panama–Pacific International Exposition"), and [1933–1934 Chicago](/wiki/Century_of_Progress "Century of Progress") were notable in this respect.[\[12]](#cite_note-12) Inventions such as the [telephone](/wiki/Telephone "Telephone") were first presented during this era. This era set the basic character of the world fair.[\[13]](#cite_note-13)



### Cultural exchange (1939–1987\)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Cultural exchange (1939–1987)")]


[Ice Follies](/wiki/Ice_Follies "Ice Follies") at the [Seattle 1962 World's Fair](/wiki/Century_21_Exposition "Century 21 Exposition")


Further information: [Technological utopianism](/wiki/Technological_utopianism "Technological utopianism")
The [1939–1940 New York World's Fair](/wiki/1939_New_York_World%27s_Fair "1939 New York World's Fair"), and those that followed, took a different approach, one less focused on technology and aimed more at cultural themes and social progress. For instance, the theme of the 1939 fair was "Building the World of Tomorrow"; at the [1964–1965 New York World's Fair](/wiki/1964_New_York_World%27s_Fair "1964 New York World's Fair"), it was "Peace Through Understanding"; at the [1967 International and Universal Exposition](/wiki/Expo_67 "Expo 67") in Montreal, it was "Man and His World". These fairs encouraged effective [intercultural communication](/wiki/Intercultural_communication "Intercultural communication") along with sharing of technological innovation.


The 1967 International and Universal Exposition in Montreal was promoted under the name *Expo 67*. Event organizers retired the term *world's fair* in favor of *Expo* (the [Montreal Expos](/wiki/Montreal_Expos "Montreal Expos"), a former [Major League Baseball](/wiki/Major_League_Baseball "Major League Baseball") team, was named for the 1967 fair).[\[14]](#cite_note-14)



### Nation branding (1988–present)

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: Nation branding (1988–present)")]


1992 Expo in Seville, Spain


From [World Expo 88](/wiki/World_Expo_88 "World Expo 88") in [Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane "Brisbane") onwards, countries started to use expositions as a platform to improve their national image through their pavilions. Finland, Japan, Canada, France, and Spain are cases in point. A major study by Tjaco Walvis called "Expo 2000 Hanover in Numbers" showed that improving [national image](/wiki/Nation_branding "Nation branding") was the main goal for 73% of the countries participating in [Expo 2000](/wiki/Expo_2000 "Expo 2000").\[*[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*] Pavilions became a kind of advertising campaign, and the Expo served as a vehicle for "nation branding". According to branding expert [Wally Olins](/wiki/Wally_Olins "Wally Olins"), Spain used [Expo '92](/wiki/Seville_Expo_%2792 "Seville Expo '92") and the [1992 Summer Olympics](/wiki/1992_Summer_Olympics "1992 Summer Olympics") in [Barcelona](/wiki/Barcelona "Barcelona") in the same year to underscore its new position as a modern and democratic country and to show itself as a prominent member of the [European Union](/wiki/European_Union "European Union") and the global community.\[*[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]


At [Expo 2000](/wiki/Expo_2000 "Expo 2000") Hanover, countries created their own architectural pavilions, investing, on average, €12 million each.[\[15]](#cite_note-15) Given these costs, governments are sometimes hesitant to participate, because the benefits may not justify the costs. However, while the effects are difficult to measure, an independent study for the Dutch pavilion at Expo 2000 estimated that the pavilion (which cost around €35 million) generated around €350 million of potential revenues for the Dutch economy. It also identified several key success factors for world\-exposition pavilions in general.[\[16]](#cite_note-16)



Types
-----

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Types")]


Expo tower for the Osaka 1970 World Expo in Japan


At present there are two types of international exhibition: World Expos (formally known as International Registered Exhibitions) and Specialised Expos (formally known as International Recognised Exhibitions).[\[17]](#cite_note-17) World Expos, previously known as universal expositions, are the biggest category events. At World Expos, participants generally build their own pavilions. They are therefore the most extravagant and most expensive expos. Their duration may be between six weeks and six months. Since 1995, the interval between two World Expos has been at least five years. World [Expo 2015](/wiki/Expo_2015 "Expo 2015") was held in Milan, Italy, from 1 May to 31 October 2015\.


Specialised Expos are smaller in scope and investments and generally shorter in duration; between three weeks and three months. Previously, these Expos were called Special Exhibitions or International Specialized Exhibitions but these terms are no longer used officially. Their total surface area must not exceed 25 hectares (62 acres) and organizers must build pavilions for the participating states, free of rent, charges, taxes and expenses. The largest country pavilions may not exceed 1,000 m2 (1⁄4 acre). Only one Specialised Expo can be held between two World Expos.[\[18]](#cite_note-18)


An additional two types of international exhibition may be recognized by the BIE: horticultural exhibitions, which are joint [BIE](/wiki/Bureau_International_des_Expositions "Bureau International des Expositions") and AIPH\-sanctioned 'garden' fairs in which participants present gardens and garden pavilions; and the semi\-regular [Milan Triennial](/wiki/Milan_Triennial "Milan Triennial") (not always held every third year) art and design exhibition, held in [Milan, Italy](/wiki/Milan,_Italy "Milan, Italy"), with the BIE granting official international exhibition status to 14 editions of the Triennale between 1996 and 2016\.[\[19]](#cite_note-19)



### World Expos

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=6 "Edit section: World Expos")]


Expo 2000 brickwork, for the World Expo in Hannover, Germany in 2000


World Expos (formally known as International Registered Exhibitions) encompass universal themes that affect the full gamut of human experience, and international and corporate participants are required to adhere to the theme in their representations. Registered expositions are held every 5 years because they are more expensive as they require total design of pavilion buildings from the ground up. As a result, nations compete for the most outstanding or memorable structure—for example Japan, France, Morocco, and Spain at [Expo '92](/wiki/Seville_Expo_%2792 "Seville Expo '92"). Sometimes prefabricated structures are used to minimize costs for developing countries, or for countries from a geographical block to share space (i.e. Plaza of the Americas at Seville '92\).


In the 21st century the BIE has moved to sanction World Expos every five years; following the numerous expos of the 1980s and 1990s, some see this as a means to cut down potential expenditure by participating nations. The move was also seen by some as an attempt to avoid conflicting with the [Summer Olympics](/wiki/Summer_Olympics "Summer Olympics"). World Expos are restricted to every five years, with Specialized Expos in the in\-between years.



### Specialised Expos

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=7 "Edit section: Specialised Expos")]


Panoramic view of [Expo 2012 Yeosu](/wiki/Expo_2012 "Expo 2012"), in South Korea


Specialized Expos (formally known as International Recognized Exhibitions) are usually united by a precise theme—such as "Future Energy" ([Expo 2017](/wiki/Expo_2017 "Expo 2017") [Astana](/wiki/Astana "Astana")), "The Living Ocean and Coast" ([Expo 2012](/wiki/Expo_2012 "Expo 2012") [Yeosu](/wiki/Yeosu "Yeosu")), or "Leisure in the Age of Technology" ([Brisbane](/wiki/Brisbane "Brisbane"), [Expo '88](/wiki/Expo_%2788 "Expo '88")). Such themes are more specific than the wider scope of world expositions.


Specialized Expos are usually smaller in scale and cheaper to run for the host committee and participants because the architectural fees are lower and they only have to customize pavilion space provided free of charge from the Organiser, usually with the prefabricated structure already completed. Countries then have the option of "adding" their own colours, design etc. to the outside of the prefabricated structure and filling in the inside with their own content.



### Horticultural Expos

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=8 "Edit section: Horticultural Expos")]


Royal Pavilion of [Royal Flora Ratchaphruek 2006](/wiki/Royal_Flora_Ratchaphruek_2006 "Royal Flora Ratchaphruek 2006"), in Chiang Mai, Thailand


Horticultural Expos (formally known as A1 International Horticultural Exhibitions) are co\-regulated by [International Association of Horticultural Producers](/wiki/International_Association_of_Horticultural_Producers "International Association of Horticultural Producers"). Like Specialised Expos are organized in a precise theme—such as "Green Desert, Better Environment" ([International Horticultural Expo 2023 Doha Qatar](/wiki/International_Horticultural_Expo_2023_Doha_Qatar "International Horticultural Expo 2023 Doha Qatar")), "Growing Green Cities" ([Floriade 2022](/wiki/Floriade_2022 "Floriade 2022")), or "Building a Beautiful Home Featuring Harmonious Coexistence between Man and Nature" ([Expo 2019](/wiki/Expo_2019 "Expo 2019")).


The purpose of these exhibitions is to foster cooperation and the sharing of knowledge and solutions between countries, horticultural producers and agricultural industries by addressing the paramount issues of healthy lifestyles, green economies, sustainable living, education and innovation.

Legacies
--------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=13 "Edit section: Legacies")]
### Remaining structures

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=14 "Edit section: Remaining structures")]


Chromolithograph of the [Eiffel Tower](/wiki/Eiffel_Tower "Eiffel Tower") illuminations in 1889




The Space Needle and Monorail depicted on this 1962 stamp


Most of the structures are temporary and are dismantled after the fair closes, except for landmark towers. By far the most famous of these is the [Eiffel Tower](/wiki/Eiffel_Tower "Eiffel Tower"), built for the [Exposition Universelle (1889\)](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1889) "Exposition Universelle (1889)"). Although it is now the most recognized symbol of its host city [Paris](/wiki/Paris "Paris"), there were contemporary critics opposed to its construction, and demands for it to be dismantled after the fair's conclusion.[\[21]](#cite_note-21)


Other structures that remain from these fairs:



* 1851 – London: [The Crystal Palace](/wiki/The_Crystal_Palace "The Crystal Palace"), from the first World's Fair in London, designed so that it could be recycled to recoup losses, was such a success that it was moved and intended to be permanent, only to be destroyed by a fire in 1936\.[\[22]](#cite_note-burning-22)
* 1876 – Philadelphia: The [Centennial Exposition](/wiki/Centennial_Exposition "Centennial Exposition")'s main building, [Memorial Hall](/wiki/Memorial_Hall_(Philadelphia) "Memorial Hall (Philadelphia)"), is still in [Fairmount Park](/wiki/Fairmount_Park "Fairmount Park"), [Philadelphia](/wiki/Philadelphia "Philadelphia"), and serving as the new home for the [Please Touch Museum](/wiki/Please_Touch_Museum "Please Touch Museum"). The space under the entrance to [Memorial Hall](/wiki/Memorial_Hall_(Philadelphia) "Memorial Hall (Philadelphia)") houses a scale model of the entire Exposition.
* 1880 – Melbourne: The [World Heritage](/wiki/World_Heritage_Site "World Heritage Site")–listed [Royal Exhibition Building](/wiki/Royal_Exhibition_Building "Royal Exhibition Building") in [Melbourne](/wiki/Melbourne "Melbourne"), constructed for the [Melbourne International Exhibition](/wiki/Melbourne_International_Exhibition_(1880) "Melbourne International Exhibition (1880)").
* 1893 – Chicago: The [Museum of Science and Industry](/wiki/Museum_of_Science_and_Industry_(Chicago) "Museum of Science and Industry (Chicago)") in [Chicago](/wiki/Chicago "Chicago") is housed in the former Palace of Fine Arts, one of the last remaining buildings of the [World's Columbian Exposition](/wiki/World%27s_Columbian_Exposition "World's Columbian Exposition"). In conjunction with the fair, the [Art Institute of Chicago](/wiki/Art_Institute_of_Chicago "Art Institute of Chicago") building was built to house conferences, as the World's Congress Auxiliary Building. The [Garden of the Phoenix](/wiki/Garden_of_the_Phoenix "Garden of the Phoenix"), a remnant of the Japanese pavilion, also survives. The intent or hope was to make all Columbian structures permanent, but most of the structures burned, possibly the result of arson during the [Pullman Strike](/wiki/Pullman_Strike "Pullman Strike"). The foundation of the world's first [Ferris wheel](/wiki/Ferris_wheel "Ferris wheel"), which operated at the Exposition, was unearthed on the [Chicago Midway](/wiki/Midway_Plaisance "Midway Plaisance") during a construction project by [the University of Chicago](/wiki/University_of_Chicago "University of Chicago"), whose campus now surrounds the Midway. Relocated survivors include the Norway pavilion, a small house now at a museum in Wisconsin, and the Maine State Building, now at the Poland Springs Resort in Maine.
* 1894 – San Francisco: The [Japanese Tea Garden](/wiki/Japanese_Tea_Garden_(San_Francisco,_California) "Japanese Tea Garden (San Francisco, California)") in [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco")'s [Golden Gate Park](/wiki/Golden_Gate_Park "Golden Gate Park") is the last major remnant of the [California Midwinter International Exposition](/wiki/California_Midwinter_International_Exposition_of_1894 "California Midwinter International Exposition of 1894"). Large ornamental wooden gates and a pagoda from the 1915 [Panama–Pacific International Exposition](/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93Pacific_International_Exposition "Panama–Pacific International Exposition") were brought in after the latter fair closed,[\[23]](#cite_note-23) making the Tea Garden a rare if not unique instance of a survivor that incorporates architectural features from two completely separate fairs.
* 1897 – Nashville: A full\-scale replica of the [Parthenon](/wiki/Parthenon_(Nashville) "Parthenon (Nashville)") was built for the [Tennessee Centennial and International Exposition](/wiki/Tennessee_Centennial_and_International_Exposition "Tennessee Centennial and International Exposition") where it stands today in [Nashville](/wiki/Nashville "Nashville")'s [Centennial Park](/wiki/Centennial_Park_(Nashville) "Centennial Park (Nashville)"). It features plaster reproductions of the [Elgin Marbles](/wiki/Elgin_Marbles "Elgin Marbles") and, in 1990, a re\-creation of the original [Athena Parthenos](/wiki/Athena_Parthenos "Athena Parthenos") statue was installed inside just as it was in the original [Parthenon](/wiki/Parthenon "Parthenon") in ancient Greece.
* 1900 – Paris: the [Grand Palais](/wiki/Grand_Palais "Grand Palais") and [Petit Palais](/wiki/Petit_Palais "Petit Palais").
* [Brookings Hall](/wiki/Brookings_Hall "Brookings Hall") at [Washington University in St. Louis](/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis"), the administration building of the [1904 World's Fair](/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase_Exposition "Louisiana Purchase Exposition")

1904 – St. Louis: The [St. Louis Art Museum](/wiki/St._Louis_Art_Museum "St. Louis Art Museum") in [Forest Park](/wiki/Forest_Park_(St._Louis,_Missouri) "Forest Park (St. Louis, Missouri)"), originally the Palace of the Fine Arts, and [Brookings Hall](/wiki/Brookings_Hall "Brookings Hall") at [Washington University in St. Louis](/wiki/Washington_University_in_St._Louis "Washington University in St. Louis"), are remnants of the [Louisiana Purchase Exposition](/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase_Exposition "Louisiana Purchase Exposition") (held a year late, as it was originally intended to be the centennial of the [Louisiana Purchase](/wiki/Louisiana_Purchase "Louisiana Purchase"). But organizers, and President [Theodore Roosevelt](/wiki/Theodore_Roosevelt "Theodore Roosevelt"), wanted the fair to be held during the Olympics which were moved from Chicago.), better known as the St. Louis World's Fair. The aviary in Forest Park gave root to the [St. Louis Zoo](/wiki/St._Louis_Zoo "St. Louis Zoo").
* 1906 – Milan: The [Civic Aquarium of Milan](/wiki/Civic_Aquarium_of_Milan "Civic Aquarium of Milan") built for the [Milan Exposition](/wiki/Milan_International_(1906) "Milan International (1906)") is still open after 100 years and was recently renovated. The [International Commission on Occupational Health](/wiki/International_Commission_on_Occupational_Health "International Commission on Occupational Health") (ICOH) was settled in Milan during the fair and had its first congress in the Expo pavilions. In June 2006 the ICOH celebrated the first century of its life in Milan. An elevated railway with trains running at short intervals linked the fair to the city center. It was dismantled in the 1920s.
* 1909 – Seattle: The landscaping (by the Olmsted brothers) from the [Alaska–Yukon–Pacific Exposition](/wiki/Alaska%E2%80%93Yukon%E2%80%93Pacific_Exposition "Alaska–Yukon–Pacific Exposition") (AYPE) in Seattle still forms much of the [University of Washington](/wiki/University_of_Washington "University of Washington") campus. The only major building left from the AYPE, Architecture Hall, is used by the university's architecture school.
* 1915 – San Francisco: The [Palace of Fine Arts](/wiki/Palace_of_Fine_Arts "Palace of Fine Arts") in [San Francisco](/wiki/San_Francisco "San Francisco") and its adjacent artificial lagoon are the only major remnants of the [Panama–Pacific International Exposition](/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93Pacific_International_Exposition "Panama–Pacific International Exposition") still in their original locations on the former fairgrounds (now the city's [Marina District](/wiki/Marina_District,_San_Francisco "Marina District, San Francisco") neighborhood), but the building is almost entirely a reconstruction. The plaster\-surfaced original, not intended to survive after the fair, was a crumbling ruin in 1964 when all but the steel framework was demolished so that it could be reproduced in concrete. The San Francisco Civic Auditorium, now the [Bill Graham Civic Auditorium](/wiki/Bill_Graham_Civic_Auditorium "Bill Graham Civic Auditorium"), is another major legacy of the fair but was built off\-site in the city's [Civic Center](/wiki/Civic_Center,_San_Francisco "Civic Center, San Francisco"). The independent [Panama–California Exposition](/wiki/Panama%E2%80%93California_Exposition "Panama–California Exposition") in [San Diego](/wiki/San_Diego "San Diego") left a substantial legacy of permanent buildings and other structures which today define its site, San Diego's central [Balboa Park](/wiki/Balboa_Park_(San_Diego) "Balboa Park (San Diego)"), including the Prado walkway, the California Tower and Dome (now home to the [Museum of Us](/wiki/San_Diego_Museum_of_Us "San Diego Museum of Us")), the 1,500\-foot Cabrillo Bridge, the lily pond and botanical gardens, and the [Spreckels Organ Pavilion](/wiki/Spreckels_Organ_Pavilion "Spreckels Organ Pavilion").[\[24]](#cite_note-24)
* 1929 – Seville and Barcelona: much survives from the two simultaneous fairs [Spain](/wiki/Spain "Spain") hosted that year. The most famous are the remnants of the [Ibero\-American Exposition](/wiki/Ibero-American_Exposition_of_1929 "Ibero-American Exposition of 1929") in [Seville](/wiki/Seville "Seville"), in which the Spanish Pavilion's *Plaza de España* forms part of a large park and forecourt. Most of that fair's pavilions have survived and been adapted for other uses, with many of them becoming [consulates\-general](/wiki/Consulate-general "Consulate-general") for the countries that built them. The [Barcelona International Exposition](/wiki/1929_Barcelona_International_Exposition "1929 Barcelona International Exposition") featured the famous [German pavilion](/wiki/Barcelona_Pavilion "Barcelona Pavilion") designed by [Mies van der Rohe](/wiki/Mies_van_der_Rohe "Mies van der Rohe"), which was demolished but later rebuilt on the original site.[\[25]](#cite_note-25)
* 1936 – Johannesburg: The [Empire Exhibition, South Africa](/wiki/Empire_Exhibition,_South_Africa "Empire Exhibition, South Africa") was built close to the [University of the Witwatersrand](/wiki/University_of_the_Witwatersrand "University of the Witwatersrand"), and by the late 1970s the growth of the university was large enough to incorporate the permanent buildings from the exhibition. In 1985, the university purchased the South African Government Building; the two Heavy Machinery Halls, now called Empire Hall and the Dining Hall; the Hall of Transport; the Tower of Light; the Cape Dutch complex; and the Bien Donne Restaurant.
* 1939 – New York City: [The New York City Building](/wiki/Queens_Museum "Queens Museum") from the [1939's World Fair](/wiki/1939_New_York_World%27s_Fair "1939 New York World's Fair"), was reused for the 1964 World's Fair and is now the Queens Museum. [Parachute jump](/wiki/Parachute_jump "Parachute jump") was a ride from the fair. It was moved to the [Coney island](/wiki/Coney_island "Coney island") boardwalk in [Brooklyn](/wiki/Brooklyn "Brooklyn").\[*[citation needed](/wiki/Wikipedia:Citation_needed "Wikipedia:Citation needed")*]
* 1942 – Rome: A special case is the [EUR](/wiki/Esposizione_Universale_Roma "Esposizione Universale Roma") quarter in [Rome](/wiki/Rome "Rome"), built for a World's Fair planned for 1942 but cancelled because of [World War II](/wiki/World_War_II "World War II"). Today it hosts governmental and private offices, and several museums.
* 1958 – Brussels: In [Brussels](/wiki/Brussels "Brussels"), the [Atomium](/wiki/Atomium "Atomium") still stands at the exposition site. It is a 165\-billion\-times\-enlarged iron\-crystal\-shaped building. Until June 2012, the "American Theatre" on the Expo grounds was frequently used as a television studio by the [VRT](/wiki/Vlaamse_Radio-_en_Televisieomroeporganisatie "Vlaamse Radio- en Televisieomroeporganisatie").




[Seattle](/wiki/Seattle "Seattle") – World's Fair sign at 47th and Aurora, 1962


* 1962 – Seattle: The [Space Needle](/wiki/Space_Needle "Space Needle") theme building of the [Century 21 Exposition](/wiki/Century_21_Exposition "Century 21 Exposition") commonly known as the Seattle World's Fair still stands as a [Seattle](/wiki/Seattle "Seattle") icon and landmark. The [Seattle Center Monorail](/wiki/Seattle_Center_Monorail "Seattle Center Monorail"), the other widely known futuristic feature of the fair, still operates daily. The US pavilion became the [Pacific Science Center](/wiki/Pacific_Science_Center "Pacific Science Center"). The original exterior and roof of the Washington State Pavilion has been preserved as a landmark, and now is part of [Climate Pledge Arena](/wiki/Climate_Pledge_Arena "Climate Pledge Arena").




The Unisphere, from the 1964 World's Fair in New York City, in the early 21st century


* 1964 – New York City: many structures still stand
	+ The [Unisphere](/wiki/Unisphere "Unisphere"), built for the [second New York World's Fair](/wiki/1964_New_York_World%27s_Fair "1964 New York World's Fair"), stands on its original site in Flushing Meadows, Queens
	+ [New York Hall of Science](/wiki/New_York_Hall_of_Science "New York Hall of Science"), built for the fair, continues to operate as a science museum, similar to its original role
	+ The Port Authority Heliport and Exhibit is now the [Terrace on the Park](/wiki/Terrace_on_the_Park "Terrace on the Park") event and catering venue
	+ The [New York State Pavilion](/wiki/New_York_State_Pavilion "New York State Pavilion") is mostly derelict, but is still an icon, with its observation towers prominently featured in 1997's *[Men in Black](/wiki/Men_in_Black_(1997_film) "Men in Black (1997 film)")*. The Theaterama building is the only portion still maintained, and is used by the [Queens Theater](/wiki/Queens_Theatre_in_the_Park "Queens Theatre in the Park"). The Tent of Tomorrow building and observation towers are being restored as of 2019[\[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit).
	+ The New York City Pavilion, a holdover form the [1939 fair](/wiki/1939_New_York_World%27s_Fair "1939 New York World's Fair"), continues to serve as the home of the [Queens Museum](/wiki/Queens_Museum "Queens Museum")
	+ Other artifacts remain throughout the park, and many buildings were transported for use elsewhere and continue to function.
* 1967 – Montreal: Among the structures still standing from Expo 67 in Montreal are [Moshe Safdie](/wiki/Moshe_Safdie "Moshe Safdie")'s [Habitat 67](/wiki/Habitat_67 "Habitat 67"), [Buckminster Fuller](/wiki/Buckminster_Fuller "Buckminster Fuller")'s American pavilion the "[Montreal Biosphere](/wiki/Montreal_Biosph%C3%A8re "Montreal Biosphère")", the Jamaica Pavilion, the Tunisia Pavilion, and the French pavilion (now the [Montreal Casino](/wiki/Montreal_Casino "Montreal Casino")).
* 1968 – San Antonio: San Antonio kept the [Tower of the Americas](/wiki/Tower_of_the_Americas "Tower of the Americas"), the [Institute of Texan Cultures](/wiki/Institute_of_Texan_Cultures "Institute of Texan Cultures") and the Convention Center from HemisFair '68\.
* 1970 – Osaka: The [Tower of the Sun](/wiki/Tower_of_the_Sun "Tower of the Sun") was left standing, but was neglected after the conclusion of the [Expo '70](/wiki/Expo_%2770 "Expo '70"). After restoration to the structure was completed, the museum inside the tower was re\-opened on 18 March 2018\.[\[26]](#cite_note-26)
* 1974 – Spokane: [Spokane](/wiki/Spokane "Spokane") still has its [Riverfront Park](/wiki/Riverfront_Park_(Spokane) "Riverfront Park (Spokane)") that was created for Expo '74—the park remains a popular and iconic part of Spokane's downtown.




View of 1982 fairgrounds, with the Sunsphere


* 1982 – Knoxville: The [Sunsphere](/wiki/Sunsphere "Sunsphere") from the [Knoxville World's Fair](/wiki/1982_World%27s_Fair "1982 World's Fair") remains as a feature of [Knoxville](/wiki/Knoxville,_Tennessee "Knoxville, Tennessee")'s skyline.
* 1984 – New Orleans: The main pavilions of the [1984 New Orleans](/wiki/1984_Louisiana_World_Exposition "1984 Louisiana World Exposition") World's Fair became the [Ernest N. Morial Convention Center](/wiki/Ernest_N._Morial_Convention_Center "Ernest N. Morial Convention Center"), which is also known for its use as a shelter of last resort during [Hurricane Katrina](/wiki/Hurricane_Katrina "Hurricane Katrina") and later hosted the [Miss Universe](/wiki/Miss_Universe "Miss Universe") in [2022](/wiki/Miss_Universe_2022 "Miss Universe 2022").
* 1986 – Vancouver: In [Vancouver](/wiki/Vancouver,_British_Columbia "Vancouver, British Columbia"), many [Expo 86](/wiki/Expo_86 "Expo 86") projects were designed as legacy projects. Of note are the [Skytrain](/wiki/Skytrain_(Vancouver) "Skytrain (Vancouver)"), [Science World](/wiki/Science_World_at_TELUS_World_of_Science "Science World at TELUS World of Science") and [Canada Place](/wiki/Canada_Place "Canada Place").
* 1988 – Brisbane: The [Skyneedle](/wiki/The_Skyneedle,_Brisbane "The Skyneedle, Brisbane"), the symbol tower of [Expo '88](/wiki/Expo_%2788 "Expo '88") in Brisbane, Queensland, Australia, still stands. Other survivors are the [Nepalese Peace Pagoda](/wiki/Nepalese_Peace_Pagoda "Nepalese Peace Pagoda") of the Nepalese representation, now at the transformed World Expo '88 site South Bank Parklands, and the Japan Pond and Garden from the Japanese representation, now at the Brisbane Mount Cooth\-tha Botanic Gardens. In 2018 the World Expo 88 Art Trail was re\-birthed and dramatically expanded as part of the 30th Anniversary of World expo 88, now forming a Major tourist attraction in its own right.[\[27]](#cite_note-27)
* 1992 – Seville: The pavilions of [Expo '92](/wiki/Seville_Expo_%2792 "Seville Expo '92") in Seville had been converted into a technological square and a [theme park](/wiki/Theme_park "Theme park").
* 1998 – Lisbon: The main buildings of [Expo '98](/wiki/Expo_%2798 "Expo '98") in [Lisbon](/wiki/Lisbon "Lisbon") were completely integrated into the city itself and many of the art exhibition pieces still remain.
* 2005 – Nagoya: The [home of Satsuki \& Mei Kusakabe](/wiki/My_Neighbor_Totoro "My Neighbor Totoro"), built for the 2005 Expo in [Aichi](/wiki/Aichi "Aichi"), remains operating at its original site in Morikoro Park and is a popular tourist attraction, eventually being incorporated into [Ghibli Park](/wiki/Ghibli_Park "Ghibli Park") in 2022\.




The [China pavilion](/wiki/China_pavilion_at_Expo_2010 "China pavilion at Expo 2010") at the [Expo 2010](/wiki/Expo_2010 "Expo 2010"); repurposed as a museum


* 2010 – Shanghai: The [China pavilion](/wiki/China_pavilion_at_Expo_2010 "China pavilion at Expo 2010") from [Expo 2010](/wiki/Expo_2010 "Expo 2010") in Shanghai, the largest display in the history of the World Expo, is now the [China Art Museum](/wiki/China_Art_Museum,_Shanghai "China Art Museum, Shanghai"), the largest art museum in Asia.
* 2015 – Milan: The Italian Pavilion of Expo 2015 remains on the original site.

### Relocation of pavilions

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=16 "Edit section: Relocation of pavilions")]


Poster for the 1900 expo


Some pavilions have been transported overseas intact:



* The Argentine Pavilion from the [1889 Paris](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1889) "Exposition Universelle (1889)") was relocated to [Buenos Aires](/wiki/Buenos_Aires "Buenos Aires"), [Argentina](/wiki/Argentina "Argentina") until its demolition in 1932\.
* The Chilean Pavilion from [1889 Paris](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1889) "Exposition Universelle (1889)") is now in [Santiago, Chile](/wiki/Santiago,_Chile "Santiago, Chile") and following significant refurbishment in 1992 functions as the Museo Artequin[\[29]](#cite_note-29)
* The [Peruvian](/wiki/Peru "Peru") Pavilion from [1900 Paris](/wiki/Exposition_Universelle_(1900) "Exposition Universelle (1900)") is now in [Lima](/wiki/Lima "Lima"), as home to the Military Academy of History.
* The [Japanese Tower](/wiki/Japanese_Tower_of_Brussels "Japanese Tower of Brussels") of the 1900 World's Fair in Paris was relocated to [Laken](/wiki/Laken "Laken") (Brussels) on request of King Leopold II of Belgium.
* The [Belgium Pavilion](/wiki/Belgian_Building "Belgian Building") from the 1939 New York World's Fair was relocated to [Virginia Union University](/wiki/Virginia_Union_University "Virginia Union University") in [Richmond, Virginia](/wiki/Richmond,_Virginia "Richmond, Virginia").
* The [USSR](/wiki/Soviet_Union "Soviet Union") Pavilion from [Expo 67](/wiki/Expo_67 "Expo 67") is now in [Moscow](/wiki/Moscow "Moscow").
* The [Sanyo](/wiki/Sanyo "Sanyo") Pavilion from [Expo '70](/wiki/Expo_%2770 "Expo '70") is the Asian Centre at the [University of British Columbia](/wiki/University_of_British_Columbia "University of British Columbia") in [Vancouver](/wiki/Vancouver "Vancouver").
* The Portugal Pavilion from [Expo 2000](/wiki/Expo_2000 "Expo 2000") is now in [Coimbra](/wiki/Coimbra "Coimbra"), [Portugal](/wiki/Portugal "Portugal").
* The United Arab Emirates Pavilion from [Expo 2010](/wiki/Expo_2010 "Expo 2010") is now in [Saadiyat Island](/wiki/Saadiyat_Island "Saadiyat Island") in [Abu Dhabi](/wiki/Abu_Dhabi "Abu Dhabi") in UAE[\[30]](#cite_note-uaenewhome-30)
* The Bahrain Pavilion from [Expo 2015](/wiki/Expo_2015 "Expo 2015") was relocated to Bahrain. The Azerbaijan Pavilion is in that country's capital [Baku](/wiki/Baku "Baku"). The Chinese Pavilion was brought back to [Qingdao](/wiki/Qingdao "Qingdao") and is on the site of the 2014 horticultural exhibition.
* The [Save the Children](/wiki/Save_the_Children "Save the Children") Italy pavilion from Expo 2015 was dismantled and re\-built as school for Syrian refugee children in [Lebanon](/wiki/Lebanon "Lebanon").[\[31]](#cite_note-catalyticactionwebsite-31)[\[32]](#cite_note-savethechildrenwebsite-32)


The Brussels [Expo '58](/wiki/Expo_%2758 "Expo '58") relocated many pavilions within [Belgium](/wiki/Belgium "Belgium"): the pavilion of Jacques Chocolats moved to the town of [Diest](/wiki/Diest "Diest") to house the new town swimming pool. Another pavilion was relocated to [Willebroek](/wiki/Willebroek "Willebroek") and has been used as dance hall Carré[\[33]](#cite_note-33) ever since. One smaller pavilion still stands on the boulevard towards the [Atomium](/wiki/Atomium "Atomium"): the restaurant "Salon 58" in the pavilion of Comptoir Tuilier.



### Other legacies

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=World%27s_fair&action=edit&section=17 "Edit section: Other legacies")]
Many exhibitions and rides created by [Walt Disney](/wiki/Walt_Disney "Walt Disney") and his [WED Enterprises](/wiki/Walt_Disney_Imagineering "Walt Disney Imagineering") company for the [1964 New York World's Fair](/wiki/1964_New_York_World%27s_Fair "1964 New York World's Fair") (which was held over into 1965\) were moved to [Disneyland](/wiki/Disneyland_Park_(Anaheim) "Disneyland Park (Anaheim)") after the closing of the Fair. Many of the rides, including "[It's a Small World](/wiki/It%27s_a_Small_World "It's a Small World")", and "[Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln](/wiki/Great_Moments_with_Mr._Lincoln "Great Moments with Mr. Lincoln")", as well as the building that housed the [Carousel of Progress](/wiki/Carousel_of_Progress "Carousel of Progress") are still in operation.[\[34]](#cite_note-OCR_s872-34) The concept of a permanent world's fair came to fruition with the Disney [Epcot](/wiki/Epcot "Epcot") theme park at the [Walt Disney World Resort](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World_Resort "Walt Disney World Resort"), near [Orlando, Florida](/wiki/Orlando,_Florida "Orlando, Florida"). Epcot has many characteristics of a typical universal exposition: national pavilions and exhibits concerning technology and/or the future, along with more typical amusement park rides. Meanwhile, several of the 1964 attractions that were relocated to Disneyland have been duplicated at the [Walt Disney World Resort](/wiki/Walt_Disney_World_Resort "Walt Disney World Resort").


Occasionally other mementos of the fairs remain. In the [New York City Subway](/wiki/New_York_City_Subway "New York City Subway") system, signs directing people to [Flushing Meadows–Corona Park](/wiki/Flushing_Meadows%E2%80%93Corona_Park "Flushing Meadows–Corona Park") remain from the 1964–1965 event. In the [Montreal Metro](/wiki/Montreal_Metro "Montreal Metro") subway at least one tile artwork of its theme, "Man and His World", remains. Also, a seemingly endless supply of souvenir items from fair visits can be found, and in the United States, at least, often turn up at garage or estate sales. Many fairs and expos produced [postage stamps](/wiki/Postage_stamp "Postage stamp") and commemorative [coins](/wiki/Coin "Coin").
