The **FIFA Women's World Cup** is an international [association football](/wiki/Association_football "Association football") competition contested by the senior [women's national teams](/wiki/List_of_women%27s_national_association_football_teams "List of women's national association football teams") of the members of Fédération Internationale de Football Association ([FIFA](/wiki/FIFA "FIFA")), the sport's international governing body. The competition has been held every four years and one year after the men's [FIFA World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_World_Cup "FIFA World Cup") since 1991, when the [inaugural tournament](/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1991 FIFA Women's World Cup"), then called the FIFA Women's World Championship, was held in China. Under the tournament's current format, national teams vie for the remaining 31 slots in a three\-year [qualification phase](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_qualification "FIFA Women's World Cup qualification"). The host nation's team is automatically entered as the first slot. The tournament, called the *World Cup Finals*, is contested at venues within the host nation(s) over about one month.


The nine FIFA Women's World Cup tournaments have been won by five national teams. The [United States](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team") have won four times. The other winners are [Germany](/wiki/Germany_women%27s_national_football_team "Germany women's national football team"), with two titles, and [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team "Japan women's national football team"), [Norway](/wiki/Norway_women%27s_national_football_team "Norway women's national football team"), and [Spain](/wiki/Spain_women%27s_national_football_team "Spain women's national football team") with one title each.


Eight countries have hosted the Women's World Cup. China and the United States have each hosted the tournament twice, while Australia, Canada, France, Germany, New Zealand, and Sweden have each hosted it once.


The [2023 competition](/wiki/2023_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2023 FIFA Women's World Cup") was hosted by Australia and New Zealand, making it the first edition to be held in the Southern Hemisphere, the first Women's World Cup to be hosted by two countries, as well as the first FIFA competition for either men or women to be held across two confederations.


The [2027 competition](/wiki/2027_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2027 FIFA Women's World Cup") will be hosted by Brazil, making it the first edition to be held in South America.




Format
------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Format")]
### Qualification

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Qualification")]
Main article: [FIFA Women's World Cup qualification](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_qualification "FIFA Women's World Cup qualification")
Qualifying tournaments are held within the six FIFA continental zones (Africa, Asia, North and Central America and Caribbean, South America, Oceania, Europe), and are organized by their respective confederations: [Confederation of African Football](/wiki/Confederation_of_African_Football "Confederation of African Football") (CAF), [Asian Football Confederation](/wiki/Asian_Football_Confederation "Asian Football Confederation") (AFC), Confederation of North, Central America, and Caribbean Association Football ([CONCACAF](/wiki/CONCACAF "CONCACAF")), South American Football Confederation ([CONMEBOL](/wiki/CONMEBOL "CONMEBOL")), [Oceania Football Confederation](/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation "Oceania Football Confederation") (OFC), and Union of European Football Associations ([UEFA](/wiki/UEFA "UEFA")). For each tournament, FIFA decides beforehand the number of berths awarded to each of the continental zones, based on the relative strength of the confederations' teams. The hosts of the World Cup receive an automatic berth in the finals. Except for the UEFA, other confederations organize its qualification campaign throughout continental tournaments. Since the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup"), the number of finalists increased from 16 to 24 and now 32\.[\[1]](#cite_note-1)



### Final tournament

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: Final tournament")]
The final tournament has featured between 12 and 32 national teams competing over about one month in the host nation(s). There are two stages: the group stage followed by the knockout stage.[\[2]](#cite_note-FIFAformat-2)


In the group stage, teams are drawn into groups of four teams each. Each group plays a [round\-robin tournament](/wiki/Round-robin_tournament "Round-robin tournament"), in which each team is scheduled for three matches against other teams in the same group. The last round of matches of each group is scheduled at the same time to preserve fairness among all four teams. In the 2015 24\-team format, the two teams finishing first and second in each group and the four best teams among those ranked third qualified for the round of 16, also called the knockout stage. Points are used to rank the teams within a group. Since [1994](/wiki/1994_FIFA_World_Cup "1994 FIFA World Cup"),  [Three points have been awarded for a win](/wiki/Three_points_for_a_win "Three points for a win"), one for a draw and none for a loss (before, winners received two points).


The ranking of each team in each group is determined as follows:[\[2]](#cite_note-FIFAformat-2)



1. Greatest number of points in group matches
2. Greatest goal difference in group matches
3. Greatest number of goals scored in group matches
4. If more than one team remains level after applying the above criteria, their ranking will be determined as follows:
	1. Greatest number of points in head\-to\-head matches among those teams
	2. Greatest goal difference in head\-to\-head matches among those teams
	3. Greatest number of goals scored in head\-to\-head matches among those teams
5. If any of the teams above remain level after applying the above criteria, their ranking will be determined by the drawing of lots


The knockout stage is a [single\-elimination tournament](/wiki/Single-elimination_tournament "Single-elimination tournament") in which teams play each other in one\-off matches, with [extra time](/wiki/Extra_time "Extra time") and [penalty shootouts](/wiki/Penalty_shootout_(association_football) "Penalty shootout (association football)") used to decide the winners if necessary. It begins with the round of 16\. This is followed by the quarter\-finals, semi\-finals, the [third\-place match](/wiki/Third_place_playoff "Third place playoff") (contested by the losing semi\-finalists), and the final.[\[2]](#cite_note-FIFAformat-2)



History
-------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=4 "Edit section: History")]
The first instance of a Women's World Cup dates back to [1970 in Italy](/wiki/1970_Women%27s_World_Cup "1970 Women's World Cup"), with the first tournament of that name taking place in July 1970, which Denmark won.[\[3]](#cite_note-3) This was followed by another unofficial World Cup tournament [in Mexico in 1971](/wiki/1971_Women%27s_World_Cup "1971 Women's World Cup"), in which [Denmark](/wiki/Denmark_women%27s_national_football_team "Denmark women's national football team") won the title after defeating [Mexico](/wiki/Mexico_women%27s_national_football_team "Mexico women's national football team"), 3–0, in the final at the [Azteca Stadium](/wiki/Estadio_Azteca "Estadio Azteca").[\[4]](#cite_note-4)[\[5]](#cite_note-5)[\[6]](#cite_note-6) In the 1980s, the [Mundialito](/wiki/Mundialito_(women) "Mundialito (women)") was held in Italy across four editions with both [Italy](/wiki/Italy_women%27s_national_football_team "Italy women's national football team") and [England](/wiki/England_women%27s_national_football_team "England women's national football team") winning two titles.[\[7]](#cite_note-7)


Several countries lifted bans on women's football in the 1970s, leading to new teams being established in many countries. After official continental women's tournaments were held [in Asia in 1975](/wiki/1975_AFC_Women%27s_Championship "1975 AFC Women's Championship")[\[8]](#cite_note-8) and [Europe in 1984](/wiki/1984_European_Competition_for_Women%27s_Football "1984 European Competition for Women's Football"), [Ellen Wille](/w/index.php?title=Ellen_Wille&action=edit&redlink=1 "Ellen Wille (page does not exist)") declared that she wanted better effort from the [FIFA Congress](/wiki/FIFA_Congress "FIFA Congress") in promoting the women's game.[\[9]](#cite_note-9) This came in the form of the [1988 FIFA Women's Invitation Tournament](/wiki/1988_FIFA_Women%27s_Invitation_Tournament "1988 FIFA Women's Invitation Tournament") in China as a test to see if a global women's World Cup was feasible. Twelve national teams took part in the competition – four from [UEFA](/wiki/UEFA "UEFA"), three from [AFC](/wiki/Asian_Football_Confederation "Asian Football Confederation"), two from [CONCACAF](/wiki/CONCACAF "CONCACAF"), one each from [CONMEBOL](/wiki/CONMEBOL "CONMEBOL"), [CAF](/wiki/Confederation_of_African_Football "Confederation of African Football") and [OFC](/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation "Oceania Football Confederation"). After the opening match of the tournament between [China](/wiki/China_women%27s_national_football_team "China women's national football team") and [Canada](/wiki/Canada_women%27s_national_soccer_team "Canada women's national soccer team") was attended by 45,000 people, the tournament was deemed a success, with crowds averaging 20,000\. [Norway](/wiki/Norway_women%27s_national_football_team "Norway women's national football team"), who was the [European champions](/wiki/1987_European_Competition_for_Women%27s_Football "1987 European Competition for Women's Football"), defeated [Sweden](/wiki/Sweden_women%27s_national_football_team "Sweden women's national football team"), 1–0, in the final, while [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil_women%27s_national_football_team "Brazil women's national football team") clinched third place by beating [the hosts](/wiki/China_women%27s_national_football_team "China women's national football team") in a penalty shootout.[\[10]](#cite_note-10) The competition was deemed a success and on 30 June FIFA approved the establishment of an official World Cup, which was to take place in [1991](/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1991 FIFA Women's World Cup") again in China. Again, twelve teams competed, this time culminating in the [United States](/wiki/United_States_women%27s_national_soccer_team "United States women's national soccer team") defeating Norway in the final, 2–1, with [Michelle Akers](/wiki/Michelle_Akers "Michelle Akers") scoring two goals.[\[11]](#cite_note-11)


The [1995 edition](/wiki/1995_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1995 FIFA Women's World Cup") in Sweden saw the experiment of a time\-out concept throughout the tournament which was later tightened mid\-tournament to only occur after a break in play. The time\-out only appeared in the one tournament which saw it scrapped. The final of the 1995 edition saw Norway, who scored 17 goals in the group stage, defeat Germany, 2–0, to capture their only title.[\[12]](#cite_note-12)
In the 1999 edition, one of the most famous moments of the tournament was American defender [Brandi Chastain](/wiki/Brandi_Chastain "Brandi Chastain")'s victory celebration after scoring the Cup\-winning [penalty kick](/wiki/Penalty_shootout_(football) "Penalty shootout (football)") against [China](/wiki/China_women%27s_national_football_team "China women's national football team"). She took off her jersey and waved it over her head (as men frequently do) as she celebrated. The 1999 final in the [Rose Bowl](/wiki/Rose_Bowl_(stadium) "Rose Bowl (stadium)") in [Pasadena, California](/wiki/Pasadena,_California "Pasadena, California"), had an attendance of 90,185\.[\[13]](#cite_note-13)


The 1999 and [2003 Women's World Cups](/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup") were both held in the United States; in 2003 China was supposed to host it, but the tournament was moved because of [SARS](/wiki/SARS "SARS").[\[14]](#cite_note-14) As compensation, China retained their automatic qualification to the 2003 tournament as host nation, and was automatically chosen to host the [2007 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup"). [Germany](/wiki/Germany "Germany") hosted the [2011 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2011 FIFA Women's World Cup"), as decided by vote in October 2007\. In March 2011, FIFA awarded [Canada](/wiki/Canada "Canada") the right to host the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup"). The 2015 competition saw the field expand from 16 to 24 teams.[\[15]](#cite_note-15)


During the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup"), both [Formiga](/wiki/Formiga_(footballer,_born_1978) "Formiga (footballer, born 1978)") of [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil_women%27s_national_football_team "Brazil women's national football team") and [Homare Sawa](/wiki/Homare_Sawa "Homare Sawa") of [Japan](/wiki/Japan_women%27s_national_football_team "Japan women's national football team") appeared in their record sixth World Cup,[\[16]](#cite_note-16) a feat that had never been achieved before by either female or male players. [Christie Pearce](/wiki/Christie_Pearce "Christie Pearce") became the oldest player to ever play in a Women's World Cup match, at the age of 40 years.[\[17]](#cite_note-17) In March 2015, FIFA awarded [France](/wiki/France "France") the right to host the [2019 FIFA Women's World Cup](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2019 FIFA Women's World Cup") over [South Korea](/wiki/South_Korea "South Korea").[\[18]](#cite_note-18)


In the 2019 edition, which was held in France, the United States won the tournament for the fourth time.


In 2023, Australia and New Zealand hosted the FIFA Women's World Cup for the first time as joint hosts, and the number of participants was expanded from 24 to 32\. It was also the first tournament to be held in the Southern Hemisphere. With [Australia](/wiki/Football_Australia "Football Australia") and [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_Football "New Zealand Football") respectively being members of the [Asian Football Confederation](/wiki/Asian_Football_Confederation "Asian Football Confederation") and [Oceania Football Confederation](/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation "Oceania Football Confederation"), this was the first FIFA senior competition to be hosted across two confederations. Spain won their first\-ever title, defeating England 1\-0 in the final. This made Spain the 2nd nation to win both the Men's and Women's World Cup, after Germany.



Trophy
------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=5 "Edit section: Trophy")]
The current trophy was designed in 1998 by William Sawaya for the 1999 tournament and takes the form of a spiral band, enclosing a football at the top. It was sculpted by Sawaya \& Moroni in [Milan](/wiki/Milan "Milan") and stands 47 cm (19 in) tall, weighs 4\.6 kg (10 lb) and is made of sterling silver clad in 23\-karat yellow and white gold.[\[19]](#cite_note-19) In the 2010s, it was fitted with a cone\-shaped base. Underneath the base, the name of each of the tournament's previous winners is engraved.[\[20]](#cite_note-20) The trophy had an estimated value in 2015 of approximately $30,000; by contrast, the men's World Cup trophy is fabricated in 18\-karat gold and has a precious metal value of $150,000\. However, a new Winner's Trophy is constructed for each women's champion to take home, while there is only one original women's trophy which is retained by FIFA with each women's champion taking home a replica trophy.[\[21]](#cite_note-21)


Since 2007, the winners are also awarded the [FIFA Champions Badge](/wiki/FIFA_Champions_Badge "FIFA Champions Badge"), which is worn on the jerseys of the winning team until the winners of the next tournament have been decided.[\[22]](#cite_note-22)

Notes

1. **[^](#cite_ref-noaet_27-0)** No [extra time](/wiki/Overtime_(sports)#Association_football "Overtime (sports)") was played.[\[26]](#cite_note-26)
2. **[^](#cite_ref-us_28-0)** The 2003 FIFA Women's World Cup was held in the United States following a [SARS](/wiki/SARS "SARS") [outbreak](/wiki/2002%E2%80%932004_SARS_outbreak "2002–2004 SARS outbreak") in China, where it was initially planned to be held.

In total, 44 nations have [played in at least one Women's World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_records_and_statistics "FIFA Women's World Cup records and statistics"). Of those, five nations have won the World Cup. With four titles, the United States is the most successful Women's World Cup team; it is one of only seven nations to [play in every World Cup](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_records_and_statistics#Overall_team_records "FIFA Women's World Cup records and statistics"). They have also had the most top\-four finishes (8\), medals (8\), as well as final appearances (5\), including the longest streak of three consecutive finals in [2011](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_final "2011 FIFA Women's World Cup final"), [2015](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_final "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup final"), and [2019](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_final "2019 FIFA Women's World Cup final").

### Best performance by confederations

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=11 "Edit section: Best performance by confederations")]
See also: [FIFA Women's World Cup performance by confederation](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_records_and_statistics#Results_by_confederation "FIFA Women's World Cup records and statistics")
As of 2023[\[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit), four of the six FIFA confederations have made it to a Women's World Cup final, the only exceptions being [CAF](/wiki/Confederation_of_African_Football "Confederation of African Football") (Africa) and the [OFC](/wiki/Oceania_Football_Confederation "Oceania Football Confederation") (Oceania). [CONMEBOL](/wiki/CONMEBOL "CONMEBOL") is the only confederation to have made a World Cup final without winning, following [Brazil](/wiki/Brazil_women%27s_national_football_team "Brazil women's national football team")'s defeat in the [2007 final](/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_final "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup final"). The farthest advancing African team was [Nigeria](/wiki/Nigeria_women%27s_national_football_team "Nigeria women's national football team"), who were eliminated in the quarter\-finals in 1999\. Oceania has sent two teams, [Australia](/wiki/Australia_women%27s_national_soccer_team "Australia women's national soccer team") and [New Zealand](/wiki/New_Zealand_women%27s_national_football_team "New Zealand women's national football team"), to the World Cup, but Australia did not advance from the group stage until after [the country's football association](/wiki/Football_Australia "Football Australia") moved to the [Asian Football Confederation](/wiki/Asian_Football_Confederation "Asian Football Confederation"), and New Zealand (which remains in the OFC) has never advanced to the knockout rounds.


The United States and Norway are the only teams to have won a tournament hosted by their own confederations, with the U.S. winning in 1999 (at home) and 2015 (in Canada), and Norway in 1995 (in Sweden).

Broadcasting and revenue
------------------------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=12 "Edit section: Broadcasting and revenue")]
As of 2017[\[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit), the [2015 FIFA Women's World Cup Final](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_Final "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup Final") was the most watched soccer match in American history with nearly 23 million viewers,[\[27]](#cite_note-ussf_070815-29) more than the [2015 NBA Finals](/wiki/2015_NBA_Finals "2015 NBA Finals") and [Stanley Cup](/wiki/Stanley_Cup "Stanley Cup").[\[28]](#cite_note-30) It was also the most watched Spanish\-language broadcast in tournament history.[\[27]](#cite_note-ussf_070815-29) More than 750 million viewers were reported to have watched the tournament worldwide.[\[29]](#cite_note-31)


The 2015 Women's World Cup generated almost $73 million.[\[30]](#cite_note-32) By comparison, that equates to 1% of the revenue generated by the [2018 men's tournament](/wiki/2018_FIFA_World_Cup "2018 FIFA World Cup") of $6\.1 billion.[\[31]](#cite_note-33)[\[32]](#cite_note-34)


In 2023, FIFA separated broadcast rights for the Women's World Cup from the men's tournament for the first time, and president [Gianni Infantino](/wiki/Gianni_Infantino "Gianni Infantino") suggested in May 2023 that the 2023 FIFA Women's World Cup might not be broadcast in the "Big 5" European countries due to disappointing offers.[\[33]](#cite_note-35)[\[34]](#cite_note-ath-2023-broadcast-36) As of 3 June 2023[\[update]](https://en.wikipedia.org/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit), FIFA had yet to reach broadcast agreements in Spain, Germany, Italy, France, Japan, or the United Kingdom, with German broadcasters [ZDF](/wiki/ZDF "ZDF") and [ARD](/wiki/ARD_(broadcaster) "ARD (broadcaster)") bidding 3% as much for the Women's World Cup as the [2022 men's World Cup](/wiki/2022_FIFA_World_Cup "2022 FIFA World Cup"), French and Spanish broadcasters less than 5%, and Italian broadcasters less than 1%.[\[34]](#cite_note-ath-2023-broadcast-36)

Awards
------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup&action=edit&section=15 "Edit section: Awards")]
Main article: [FIFA Women's World Cup awards](/wiki/FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup_awards "FIFA Women's World Cup awards")
At the end of each World Cup, awards are presented to select players and teams for accomplishments other than their final team positions in the tournament.



* There are currently five post\-tournament awards from the FIFA Technical Study Group:[\[35]](#cite_note-37)
	+ The **Golden Ball** (currently commercially termed "[adidas](/wiki/Adidas "Adidas") Golden Ball") for the best overall player of the tournament (first awarded in [1991](/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1991 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **Golden Boot** (currently commercially termed "Adidas Golden Boot", formerly known as the *Golden Shoe*) for the top goalscorer of the tournament (first awarded in [1991](/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1991 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **Golden Glove** (currently commercially termed "Adidas Golden Glove", formerly known as the *Best Goalkeeper*) for the best goalkeeper of the tournament (first awarded in [2003](/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **FIFA Young Player Award** for the best player of the tournament under 21 years of age at the start of the calendar year (first awarded in [2011](/wiki/2011_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2011 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **FIFA Fair Play Trophy** for the team with the best record of fair play during the tournament (first awarded in [1991](/wiki/1991_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1991 FIFA Women's World Cup")).


* There is currently one award voted on by fans during the tournament:
	+ The **Player of the Match** (currently commercially termed "[VISA](/wiki/Visa_Inc. "Visa Inc.") Player of the Match") for outstanding performance by a player during each match of the tournament (first awarded in [2003](/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup")).


* There is currently one award voted on by fans after the conclusion of the tournament:
	+ The **Goal of the Tournament** (currently commercially termed "[Hyundai](/wiki/Hyundai_Motor_Company "Hyundai Motor Company") Goal of the Tournament") for the fans' best goal scored during the tournament (first awarded in [2007](/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup")).


* The following five awards are no longer given:
	+ The **All\-Star Squad** for the best squad of players of the tournament (chosen by the technical study group, awarded from [1999](/wiki/1999_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "1999 FIFA Women's World Cup") to [2015](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **Most Entertaining Team** for the team that entertained the fans the most during the tournament (voted on by fans after the conclusion of the tournament, awarded in [2003](/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup") and [2007](/wiki/2007_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2007 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **FANtasy All\-Star Team** for the fans' best eleven\-player line\-up of the tournament (voted on by fans after the conclusion of the tournament, awarded in [2003](/wiki/2003_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2003 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The **Dream Team** for the fans' best manager and eleven\-player line\-up of the tournament (voted on by fans after the conclusion of the tournament, awarded in [2015](/wiki/2015_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2015 FIFA Women's World Cup"));
	+ The  **Players Who Dared to Shine** for ten key players of the tournament who "dared to shine" (chosen by the technical study group, awarded in [2019](/wiki/2019_FIFA_Women%27s_World_Cup "2019 FIFA Women's World Cup")).
