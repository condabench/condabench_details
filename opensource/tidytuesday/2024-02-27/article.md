**February 29** is a *[leap day](/wiki/Leap_day "Leap day")* (or "leap year day")—an [intercalary date](/wiki/Intercalation_(timekeeping) "Intercalation (timekeeping)") added periodically to create [leap years](/wiki/Leap_year "Leap year") in the [Julian](/wiki/Julian_calendar "Julian calendar") and [Gregorian calendars](/wiki/Gregorian_calendar "Gregorian calendar"). It is the 60th day of a leap year in both Julian and Gregorian calendars, and 306 days remain until the end of the leap year. It is the last day of [February](/wiki/February "February") in leap years and does not exist in common years. It is also the last day of [meteorological winter](/wiki/Meteorological_winter "Meteorological winter") in the [Northern Hemisphere](/wiki/Northern_Hemisphere "Northern Hemisphere") and the last day of [meteorological summer](/wiki/Meteorological_summer "Meteorological summer") in the [Southern Hemisphere](/wiki/Southern_Hemisphere "Southern Hemisphere") in leap years.


In the Gregorian calendar, the standard [civil calendar](/wiki/Civil_calendar "Civil calendar") used in most of the world, February 29 is added in each year that is an integer multiple of four, unless it is evenly divisible by 100 but not by 400\. For example, [1900](/wiki/1900 "1900") was not a leap year, but [2000](/wiki/2000 "2000") was. The Julian calendar—[since 1923](/wiki/Adoption_of_the_Gregorian_calendar#Adoption_in_Eastern_Europe "Adoption of the Gregorian calendar") a [liturgical calendar](/wiki/Liturgical_calendar "Liturgical calendar")—has a February 29 every fourth year without exception. Consequently, February 29 in the Julian calendar, since 1900, falls 13 days later than February 29 in the Gregorian, until the year 2100\.[\[1]](#cite_note-1)


The convention of using February 29 was not widely accepted before the 15th century; from Julian's edict until the 16th century (formally), [February 24 was doubled](/wiki/Bissextile_day "Bissextile day") instead.[\[2]](#cite_note-2) In [one of its attempts to adopt the Gregorian calendar](/wiki/Adoption_of_the_Gregorian_calendar#Sweden "Adoption of the Gregorian calendar"), Sweden tried (unsuccessfully) to phase in the change by omitting leap days in the 11 successive leap\-years, 1700–1740\.[\[3]](#cite_note-Lamont-3)




Events
------

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=February_29&action=edit&section=1 "Edit section: Events")]
### Pre\-1600

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=February_29&action=edit&section=2 "Edit section: Pre-1600")]
* [888](/wiki/AD_888 "AD 888") – [Odo](/wiki/Odo_of_France "Odo of France"), [count of Paris](/wiki/Count_of_Paris "Count of Paris"), is crowned king of [West Francia](/wiki/West_Francia "West Francia") (France) by Archbishop [Walter of Sens](/wiki/Walter_(archbishop_of_Sens) "Walter (archbishop of Sens)") at [Compiègne](/wiki/Compi%C3%A8gne "Compiègne").[\[4]](#cite_note-4)
* [1504](/wiki/1504 "1504") – [Christopher Columbus](/wiki/Christopher_Columbus "Christopher Columbus") uses his knowledge of [a lunar eclipse that night](/wiki/March_1504_lunar_eclipse "March 1504 lunar eclipse") to convince Jamaican natives to provide him with supplies.[\[5]](#cite_note-5)


### 1601–1900

\[[edit](/w/index.php?title=February_29&action=edit&section=3 "Edit section: 1601–1900")]
